## The short version

This paper covers the first half of Unit 7: what density is, how you measure it for a liquid, for a
regular solid and for an irregular one, the three states of matter, and plasma. That is exactly the
ground her Aasan Notes Part 7A and Part 7B cover. The chapter has not been taught in class and the paper
says so on its face in Roman Urdu, without naming any date. Two thirds of the paper is that new material
and the rest is repair work on mistakes she has already made, with Unit 6 section 6.5 Efficiency as the
big revision question.

The version dated 22 September was built when the chapter scan was still missing her book p170 and p171,
so it could not touch **density of liquids** or the **volume formulas for a cuboid, a cube and a sphere**.
Both of those are now in, because the complete chapter is in the repo and because Part 7A taught her
exactly that material. Four of the book's six end-of-chapter numericals need one or the other, so this
was the real gap.

The other change is the new rule that **the three quiz questions inside each note must appear on that
subject's paper**. All six, from Part 7A and Part 7B, are on this paper with the same numbers she
practised. They are worth 13 of the 27 new marks, so doing the quizzes pays off directly.

Shape: 38 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes, no choices anywhere. 10 MCQs, 3 short questions of 4 marks, 2 long
questions of 8 marks. New content 27 marks (71%), revision 11 marks (29%).

## Traps avoided

- **Petrol is never asked.** Table 7.1 says 800 kg/m3 while the her-p168 box works from 0.9 g/cm3. Q3's
  answer does come out at 800 kg/m3, which is petrol's Table 7.1 value, but the question never asks her to
  name the liquid and the key tells the marker to accept the name without reward or penalty if she adds it
  herself.
- **Osmium is never asked.** The "Do you know" box calls osmium harder than diamond, which is false, and
  no SLO asks for the densest material.
- **Figure 7.4 is never used.** In her printing its Solids and Gases panels are swapped, so a diagram
  question off it would teach her the wrong thing. Q4 is set from Table 7.2 and the running text instead.
- **Nothing from 7.5 onwards** (temperature, absolute zero, internal energy, thermometers). That is part 2.
- **The book's own numericals on her p188 are not copied.** Its cooking oil is 500 mL and 450 g, its
  plastic box is 70 x 10 x 30 cm and 2500 g, its iron cube is 10 cm and half a kilogram, its stone is 200 g
  rising 40 to 73 mL. This paper uses 120 g, 50 mL, 160 g; 20 x 10 x 5 cm, 19.3 kg; and 175 g rising 30 to
  55 mL.
- **The book's worked examples are not copied either.** Example 7.1 is a 5 cm cube of 500 g, Example 7.2 is
  145 g, 60 mL, 205 g, Example 7.4 is 80.52 g in 18 to 46 mL. All different here.
- **No scenario or activity framing.** Every written question uses the book's own style: define, write,
  calculate, compare, explain, name.
- **Physics content only**, and every item is from her own Physics textbook.

## Answer leaks: two found and fixed during this rebuild

1. **MCQ 1 said "Written in SI units, this density is:"** while Q2(i) asks her to give the SI unit of
   density. The MCQ's own stem handed her the answer. MCQ 1 now runs the other way, kg/m3 into g/cm3, and
   never uses the words "SI unit".
2. **A draft MCQ 3 asked which apparatus measures the volume of a liquid.** Q3's stem says "50 mL of a
   liquid is then poured into the cylinder", which answers it outright. MCQ 3 now asks whether density is a
   scalar quantity, which nothing else on the paper touches.

The two leaks fixed in the September version are still fixed: the HIDAYAAT worked example still uses a
neutral 2.5 g/cm3, a value that is not the answer to any question on this paper, and no question's answer
is printed in another question's stem.

Judgement calls deliberately left in, all of them mild:

- **kg/m3 appears in three stems** (MCQ 1's options, Q3(iii), Q5(B)(v)) while Q2(i) asks for the SI unit of
  density. Nothing on the paper calls it the SI unit, so she still has to know that, but a determined
  guesser could infer it. Unavoidable on a density paper.
- **MCQ 1 converts in the opposite direction from Q3(iii).** Both use the factor of 1000, which is
  reinforcement rather than an answer.
- **Q2(iii) and Q5(B)(vi) both name a metal** (iron in the stem, gold as the answer). Iron is 7900 and gold
  is 19300, far apart, so the priming does not mislead.
- **MCQ 7's stem mentions electrons being removed**, which supports MCQ 6's answer about free electrons and
  moving ions. Same-topic reinforcement, and it was in the twice-verified September version.
- **Q4(i) and Q5(B)(vi) both ask her to recall from a printed table** (Table 7.2 and Table 7.1). Both are
  named in the question, and both tables are in her book.
