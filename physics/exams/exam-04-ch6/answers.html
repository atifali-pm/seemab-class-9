SECTION A — Multiple Choice Questions (12 × 1 = 12 Marks)
1. C — Joule 1 mark
Work is defined as W = Fd (force times displacement in the direction of force). Its SI unit is the joule (J), where 1 J = 1 N·m. Watt is the unit of power, pascal is pressure, and newton is force.
2. C — Zero 1 mark
Work done W = F × d. Since the wall does not move, displacement d = 0, therefore W = 0. No matter how large the force, work is only done when there is displacement in the direction of the force. The man uses energy (metabolic), but does no mechanical work on the wall.
3. B — 25 J 1 mark
Ep = mgh. Weight W = mg = 5 N, height h = 5 m. Therefore Ep = 5 N × 5 m = 25 J. (The weight in newtons already equals mg, so we use Ep = W × h = 5 × 5 = 25 J.) Common error: multiplying mass in kg; the question gives weight in newtons.
4. B — Speed 1 mark
Kinetic energy Ek = ½mv². It depends on mass and the square of speed (velocity). Height is linked to potential energy, weight is force due to gravity, and pressure is not involved in kinetic energy formula.
5. B — 50 J 1 mark
By conservation of energy (no friction), KE at the ground = PE at maximum height = ½mv² = ½ × 4 × 5² = ½ × 4 × 25 = 50 J. The energy returns to kinetic form when the ball returns to ground level. This is the work-kinetic energy theorem in action.
6. B — Change forms 1 mark
The Law of Conservation of Energy states: "Energy can neither be created nor destroyed in any process; it can be converted from one form to another but the total amount of energy remains constant." Options A and D describe dissipation (a real phenomenon), but the law says energy changes forms, not that it is truly lost.
7. C — Watt 1 mark
Power P = W/t. Its SI unit is the watt (W), named in honour of James Watt, a Scottish physicist who invented the first steam engine. 1 W = 1 J s−1. Horsepower is a non-SI unit (1 hp = 746 W).
8. B — 20 W 1 mark
P = W/t = 100 J / 5 s = 20 W. 10 W would result from 100 J in 10 s; 105 W is a common addition error; 500 W = 100 × 5 (multiplication instead of division).
9. D — 746 W 1 mark
One horsepower (hp) = 550 ft·lb/s = 746 W (exactly). This is a standard conversion from the textbook. 756, 716, and 736 are common distractors from approximate or incorrect conversions.
10. C — Sunlight 1 mark
Sunlight (solar radiation) is a renewable energy resource; it is replenished continuously by the Sun. Coal and natural gas are fossil fuels (non-renewable). Uranium is used as nuclear fuel and is also non-renewable because the material itself (U-235) is finite and not naturally replenished on a human timescale.
11. C — 30% 1 mark
η = (Eout/Ein) × 100% = (120/400) × 100% = 0.3 × 100% = 30%. This is Example 6.6 from the textbook. The engine wastes 280 J as heat and other forms of energy, keeping only 120 J as useful work.
12. D — 1 (100%) 1 mark
A practical engine cannot have efficiency equal to or greater than 1 (100%) because frictional forces always dissipate some energy as heat. Work output is always less than work input. This would violate the laws of thermodynamics (a perpetual motion machine concept). Efficiency is always < 1 in the real world.

Q.2(iv) — Main Question 3 marks
State the Law of Conservation of Energy. Using a falling ball, explain the KE/PE interchange.

Law: "Energy can neither be created nor destroyed in any process. It can be converted from one form to another, but the total amount of energy remains constant." Mathematically: ETi = ETf. 1 mark

Falling ball example: 2 marks

  • At top (initial point): Ek = 0, Ep = mgh. Total energy = mgh.
  • During fall: As height decreases, Ep decreases; Ek increases. At any point: Ek + Ep = mgh (constant).
  • Just before ground (final point): Ep = 0, Ek = mgh. Total energy = mgh. Energy is conserved throughout.

What is a perpetual motion machine? Why is it impossible? Name the two laws it violates.

Perpetual motion is the motion of bodies that continues forever in an unperturbed system. A perpetual motion machine is a hypothetical machine that can do work indefinitely without an external energy source. 1 mark

It is impossible because every real process dissipates some energy as heat due to friction and other losses. Work output is always less than work input. 1 mark

3. Derivation of Ek = ½mv² (graphical method): 1 mark

Consider a constant force F on mass m, starting from rest (vi = 0), reaching velocity v over displacement d. From the force-displacement graph, the area under the graph is a rectangle:

Work done = Area = F × d

Since average velocity vavg = v/2 and acceleration a = v/t, displacement d = vavg × t = (v/2)(t). Also F = ma = m(v/t). Therefore:

W = F × d = m(v/t) × (v/2)(t) = ½mv²

Ek = ½mv²

OR
Describe three renewable energy resources (hydroelectric, solar, wind): energy conversion, electricity generation, one advantage and one disadvantage each. Then: find time for 8.13 kW pump to lift 2500 kg of water 100 m. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

1. Hydroelectric Energy: 1 mark

Conversion: Gravitational PE of water → KE → electrical energy.

Generation: Water stored at height in a reservoir behind a dam falls through intakes, spins turbines connected to generators, producing electricity.

Advantage: Renewable and highly efficient (95%); once built, minimal environmental operation impact. Disadvantage: Large reservoirs flood farmland and change ecosystems.

2. Solar Radiation: 1 mark

Conversion: Solar (radiant) energy → electrical energy (via photovoltaic cells) or thermal energy (via solar thermal systems).

Generation: Solar photovoltaic cells (silicon semiconductors) convert sunlight directly into electricity. CSP systems concentrate sunlight to heat a fluid, produce steam, drive turbines.

Advantage: Completely free, renewable, emits no greenhouse gases. Disadvantage: Intermittent (depends on sunlight); significant land area required.

3. Wind Energy: 1 mark

Conversion: KE of wind → rotational KE of turbine blades → electrical energy.

Generation: Wind turbines have large rotor blades connected to a hub, gearbox, and generator. Blades spin due to wind, the generator produces alternating current (AC). Wind farms group turbines in areas with consistent strong winds.

Advantage: Eco-friendly, renewable, zero greenhouse gas emissions during operation. Disadvantage: Only viable in areas with constant strong winds; infrastructure is expensive.
