Hello Seemab. Today's scene is also in Masjid-e-Nabawi, but this time what happens will startle you.

A man from the desert, a villager, comes into the mosque and urinates in one corner of it. Inside Masjid-e-Nabawi, where the prayer is held.

Think of the anger of the companions, may Allah be pleased with them. You know very well what would happen if somebody did this in one of our mosques today.

Now see what the Prophet, peace be upon him, did. He stopped the people. Then he asked for a bucket of water, and the mosque was cleaned. No shouting, and no scolding.

The man himself described what happened next. He came to me, he did not scold me and he did not speak harshly to me, he only said that this mosque is a place in which one does not urinate, it was built for the remembrance of Allah and for prayer. Sunan Ibn Majah, hadith number five hundred and twenty nine.

That man understood the religion right there, and he told the story for the rest of his life. If he had been scolded that day, he would have run out and never come back. That is what training people really means.

The basic principle in your book is that he acted on a thing himself first, and then people made that action a part of their own lives. Two qualities stand out: wisdom and insight. He kept in mind what the listener was capable of, he never spoke out of place, and he paid particular attention to the time and the occasion. Fix three words in your mind: acting first, wisdom, insight.

Now seven methods, each one with its own incident.

First. Correcting without naming anyone. He would not train someone by naming them in public or by interrupting them over a mistake; he would give the advice in a general way instead.

Second. Gentleness and love. This is the villager's incident, Sunan Ibn Majah, hadith number five hundred and twenty nine. When someone had to be warned immediately, he would explain with the greatest gentleness, so that the listener became willing to accept the truth.

Third. Kindness, and never looking for faults. Hazrat Anas bin Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, says: I served the Prophet, peace be upon him, for ten years, and whatever I did in whatever manner I did it, he never once said why did you do this, and for what I could not do, he never said why did you not do this. Sahih Muslim, hadith number two thousand three hundred and nine. Ten years, a child, and not a single scolding.

Fourth. Brevity, that is, jawami ul kalim. He avoided long sermons and would express great matters in short sentences. Allah had granted him jawami ul kalim, the quality of comprehensive speech.

Fifth. Regard for temperament and state of mind. A man asked him, when will the Day of Judgement come. He saw that the man was gripped by worry about that Day, so instead of answering he asked a question back: what have you prepared for it? Sahih Bukhari, hadith number six thousand one hundred and sixty seven. That graceful reply turned the questioner towards examining his own self.

Sixth. Encouragement. A man came to meet him. He noticed the marks on the man's palms and asked about them. The man said, I am a labourer, these marks come from hard work. Hearing that, he took the man's hands with affection and kissed them. The rough hands of a labourer, and the Prophet, peace be upon him, kissing them. Actions like this create love in the heart, and a person then stays on that path in every condition.

Seventh. Gradualness, step by step. He would remove one fault and nurture one virtue. When sending Hazrat Muadh bin Jabal, may Allah be pleased with him, to Yemen he said: Muadh, you are going to the People of the Book, so first invite them to the oneness of Allah and to the prophethood. When they accept that, tell them Allah has made five prayers obligatory in the day and night. Then tell them that zakat is obligatory, taken from their wealthy and distributed among their own poor. Sahih Bukhari, hadith number one thousand three hundred and ninety five. Not everything on one day, but one thing after another.

Now apply this to your own life. If a friend makes a mistake, do not say her name in front of the whole class, take her aside and say it there. If Arsh breaks something, let your first sentence not be why did you do that. And when somebody corrects you gently, understand that they are following the Sunnah.

Now the exam. The exercise for this lesson is on page seventy eight of your book, and the three questions in question two are the same ones that are in your quiz.

First. Regard for temperament and state of mind, with an example. The whole incident about the Day of Judgement, the question asked back in the same way, the reference Sahih Bukhari, and the outcome, that the questioner turned to examining his own self.

Second. In what manner did he speak for correction and training. Four things: never speaking out of place, advising without naming anyone, gentleness and love, and brevity, that is jawami ul kalim.

Third. How did he encourage the companions, may Allah be pleased with them, with an example. The whole incident of the labourer, the kissing of the hands, and the closing sentence that this creates love in the heart.

And one thing to carry for life. The point is to put the matter right, not to break the person. Saying the truth is easy. Saying it in a way that the other person can accept it, that is real training.
