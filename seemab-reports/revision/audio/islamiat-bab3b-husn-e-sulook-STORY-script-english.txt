Hello Seemab. Today's lesson will not begin with a definition. Today we walk straight into a scene, in Madina Munawwara.

Picture it. Beside Masjid-e-Nabawi there is a small room. Inside, the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, is seated. The same person at whose single word the whole of Arabia would rise to its feet.

There is a sound at the door. Hazrat Fatima az-Zahra, may Allah be pleased with her, comes in. His own daughter.

What would you expect to happen. The daughter arrives, the father stays seated, gives a smile, and that is that. In our own homes this is usually what happens.

But what actually happened is described by Hazrat Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her, herself. He would stand up. He would welcome her. He would take her hand. He would kiss her forehead. And he would seat her in his own place. The reference is given in your book itself: Shuab ul Iman, hadith number eight thousand nine hundred and twenty seven.

Stop and look at that scene again. The greatest man in the world is standing up for his daughter. Four actions, and every one of them visible to others. That is the heart of this lesson: respect is not something you keep inside your heart, it is something you show by doing.

This was not a coincidence, it was obedience to a command. Allah says in the Holy Quran: wa aashiroohunna bil maroof. The translation is, and live with them in kindness. This is verse nineteen of Surah An-Nisa. Learn it by heart, because every answer in this topic will begin with it.

Now four sayings, each one with its reference.

First. The best of you are those who are best to their families, and I am the best of you to my family. Jami Tirmizi, hadith number three thousand eight hundred and ninety five.

Second. Whoever raises two daughters until they reach adulthood. Then the Prophet, peace be upon him, joined his two fingers together and said: he and I will be this close on the Day of Judgement. Sahih Muslim, hadith number six thousand six hundred and ninety five. Simply for raising daughters well, the company of the Prophet, peace be upon him, in Paradise.

Third. Whoever has a daughter, and does not bury her alive, nor humiliate her, nor favour his son over her, Allah will admit him into Paradise because of her. Sunan Abi Dawood, hadith number five thousand one hundred and forty six.

Fourth, and the most beautiful of them. Hold fast to the service of your mother, because Paradise lies beneath her feet. Sunan Nasai, hadith number three thousand one hundred and six.

Now two more scenes, both small, both impossible to forget.

Hazrat Halima Sadia, may Allah be pleased with her, was his foster mother. When she came to visit, he spread out his own sheet on the ground in her honour, and sat talking with her for a long time. She had nursed him when he was a baby. Years later, the Prophet of all Arabia spread his own sheet on the ground for her.

And the third. The women said that the men had got ahead of them because they learned more from him, and asked him to fix a day for them as well. He promised them a day, and on that day he would meet the women and teach them. A whole day for the education of girls, in Madina, fourteen hundred years ago.

Now connect this to your own life, Seemab.

Your book states clearly what must not be done. Calling out at women, staring at them, making fun of them, loitering without reason outside women's educational institutions, and sitting in their pathways. All five of these are to be avoided. And what must be done: modesty, good manners and high character in public places, and extra respect for working women, because they are carrying more than their own share of responsibility.

What does that mean inside your school. When somebody laughs at a girl's face, at her clothes or at something she said, that is not merely bad manners. It goes against the very principle that the Prophet, peace be upon him, stood up for himself. And at home, when the brother is served first and the sister afterwards, remember the third hadith.

Now two minutes on the exam. The exercise for this lesson is on pages seventy four and seventy five of your book, and the three short questions in question two are the same ones that are in your quiz.

First. What treatment did Fatima az-Zahra, may Allah be pleased with her, receive on arriving, with the reference. Count all four actions: standing up, taking her hand, kissing her forehead, seating her in his own place. Then the reference. Leave the reference out and you lose half a mark.

Second. What was said about the one who raises two daughters. Be sure to write the sentence about joining the two fingers, otherwise the answer is incomplete, and the reference is Sahih Muslim.

Third. What steps are there for the respect of women, and why are they needed. The five things to avoid, then the sentence about public places, and at the end the reason: without respect for women no society can travel the road of progress. Without that last sentence the answer is not complete.

And one thing to carry for life. Respect is not what you keep in your heart. Respect is what you stand up and show.

Goodbye, Seemab. Next time a man from the desert will do something inside Masjid-e-Nabawi that will bring every companion to his feet in anger, and the Prophet's response, peace be upon him, will astonish you.
