Hello Seemab. Today there is no new lesson. Today there are only the three things that were missing from your last paper. But first, a question.

Imagine a boy of about ten. About your brother Arsh's age. For ten years, ten whole years, he works in somebody's household. Fetching water, carrying messages, small errands.

Now think how many times in ten years something must have gone wrong. How many times something got broken, or a task was forgotten, or a message was carried to the wrong person. A hundred times? A thousand?

So tell me, in those ten years, how many times was he scolded?

The answer is: not once. Not a single time. And that boy was Hazrat Anas bin Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, and that household was the household of the Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him. Hold that thought, we will come back to it.

The first thing. Kindness, and never looking for faults.

Hazrat Anas says it himself, and learn these words, because these are the ones you write in the exam: I served the Prophet, peace be upon him, for ten years. Whatever I did, in whatever manner I did it, he never once said to me, why did you do this? And for whatever I could not do, he never said, why did you not do this?

The reference is Sahih Muslim, hadith two thousand three hundred and nine.

Now notice something. There are two sentences here, and both of them have to be written. The first: why did you do this. The second: why did you not do this. One is about the things he did, the other about the things he failed to do. If you write only one, the answer is only half there.

And connect it to your own life. When a small child breaks something in front of you, what is the first sentence out of anyone's mouth? Why did you do that. That is precisely the sentence that was never once spoken in ten years.

The second thing. Brevity, which in Arabic is called jawami ul kalim.

The book says: in matters of correction and training he avoided long sermons and lengthy admonition, and would express great matters in short sentences. Allah had granted him jawami ul kalim, that is, the special quality of comprehensive speech.

In plain words: a very large meaning inside a very few words.

Think of a friend who takes half an hour to explain one thing, and another who says it in a single sentence and you understand at once. Which one do you remember afterwards? The second one. That is jawami ul kalim.

In the exam, write the words jawami ul kalim exactly as they are, and write the meaning beside them: comprehensive speech.

The third thing, and this one also came up as a multiple choice question on your paper. Tadrij.

The book's sentence is this, and learn it word for word: the Prophet, peace be upon him, observed tadrij in every task and every matter. He would carefully weigh the strengths and the weaknesses of the person being trained, removing one fault and nurturing one virtue.

Tadrij means step by step. Not everything on one day, but one thing after another.

And its example is the memorable one. He sent Hazrat Muadh bin Jabal, may Allah be pleased with him, to Yemen. Yemen, remember that word, because that is exactly what the question asked. And he said:

Muadh, you are going to the People of the Book. First invite them to the oneness of Allah and to the prophethood. When they accept that, then tell them that Allah has made five prayers obligatory in the day and the night. And when they accept that as well, then tell them that zakat is obligatory upon them, taken from their wealthy and distributed among their own poor.

The reference is Sahih Bukhari, hadith one thousand three hundred and ninety five.

Remember the three steps: oneness and prophethood, then prayer, then zakat. The order does not change.

And think how sensible this is. If everything had been said on the first day, people would have been overwhelmed. So, one thing after another. Your own studying works the same way: one lesson an evening, not a whole unit in one night.

Now back to that boy of ten. You can answer it yourself. Ten years, thousands of tasks, and not once why did you do this, nor why did you not do this. That is kindness, and that is what it means to refrain from fault-finding.

Remember this for life: the point is to put the matter right, not to break the person.

Now the exam technique, two minutes of it. When the eight-mark question comes asking for a note on his method of training, seven methods have to be written. Four you already know: correcting without naming anyone, gentleness and love, regard for temperament and state of mind, and encouragement. And today's three: kindness with no fault-finding, brevity or jawami ul kalim, and tadrij.

And each one with its incident and its reference. Writing the heading alone is not enough. That is exactly why marks were lost last time.
