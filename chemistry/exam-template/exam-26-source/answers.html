2. Definitions and composition   4 marks   (Notes Quiz 1)
Atmosphere (1): "The envelope of gases and water vapour surrounding the planet Earth is called the atmosphere."
Pollutant (1): "Anything that is in the air, water or soil which has a harmful effect on some part of the environment is called pollutant." All three of air, water and soil are needed; naming only air scores ½.
Nitrogen 78% and oxygen 21% (1). Water vapour up to 4% in a tropical rainforest (1).
3. The six pollutants, SOx and NOx   4 marks   (Notes Quiz 2)
The six (1½, ¼ each): sulphur oxides, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, methane, ground level ozone, particulates. (Figure 11.2)
Sulphur oxides (1¼): SO2 / SO3, written SOx (¼). Source, any one (½): burning fossil fuels in power plants, vehicles, industrial units, power generators, residential heating. Effects, any two (½): powerful irritant absorbed in the respiratory system; aggravates asthma, bronchitis, emphysema; causes acid rain; causes haze.
Nitrogen oxides (1¼): NO and NO2, written NOx (¼). Source, any one (½): burning fossil fuels in vehicles, power plants, industrial units, generators; car engines produce a great deal. Effects, any two (½): highly toxic; acid rain; photochemical smog; headache; respiratory problems.
4. Carbon monoxide and ground level ozone   4 marks   (Notes Quiz 3)
(i) 1½: By incomplete combustion, when there is not enough oxygen in the combustion process (¾). It cannot be detected because it is a colourless and odourless gas, so its presence cannot be felt (¾). Both words are needed; one alone scores ½.
(ii) 1½: It combines with haemoglobin to form carboxyhaemoglobin (¾, the name is compulsory), which is unable to carry oxygen, so the person loses consciousness, suffocates, and it can cause death (¾).
(iii) 1: Because it is not emitted directly. It is produced by complex reactions between nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds (VOC) under the influence of sunlight.

(ii) 2 marks: Incomplete combustion means burning that happens when there is not enough oxygen in the combustion process (1). It produces carbon monoxide (1), and also particulate matter.
(iii) 2 marks: As the heater keeps burning in a sealed room it uses up the oxygen, so combustion becomes more and more incomplete and more carbon monoxide is produced (1). Because CO is colourless and odourless nobody notices, and it forms carboxyhaemoglobin so the person suffocates. Precaution (1): keep a window or ventilator open so fresh air can enter; do not leave a heater burning in a closed room overnight.
Note for the marker: this part is deliberately open. Any precaution that supplies ventilation earns the mark.
6. [REVISION, Unit 8] Enthalpy and bond energy   8 marks
(i) 2 marks: "The amount of heat or thermal energy evolved or absorbed in a chemical reaction is called enthalpy of reaction" (1). Its sign is negative for exothermic and positive for endothermic reactions (1, ½ each). Source p131.
(ii) 1 mark: ΔH° = Sum of bond dissociation energies of reactants − Sum of bond energies of products. Source p131, section 8.4.
(iii) 3 marks. Full working:
ΔH° = [B.E. of H–H + B.E. of F–F] − [2 × B.E. of H–F]   (formula, 1)
ΔH° = [436 + 155] − [2 × 567]   (substitution, 1)
ΔH° = 591 − 1134
ΔH° = −543 kJ/mol   (answer with unit, 1; ½ off if the unit is missing)
This is Concept Assessment Exercise 8.2 from her book, p132, never previously set. The method is Example 8.1 on the same page, which worked H2 + I2 → 2HI to −11 kJ/mol.
(iv) 1 mark: Exothermic, because ΔH° is negative. Saying exothermic without the reason scores ½.
(v) 1 mark. THIS IS THE ITEM THE WHOLE REVISION SECTION EXISTS FOR.
For 2HF(g) → H2(g) + F2(g),   ΔH° = +543 kJ/mol.
Reason: reversing a reaction keeps the same magnitude and changes the sign. The energy released when those bonds formed must be supplied again to break them, so the reverse reaction is endothermic.
Why this is here: on Chemistry exam-25 her single largest loss was Q.7, where a reaction was reversed and she reused the worked example's value instead of re-deriving the direction. A student who writes −543 here, or who recalculates from scratch and gets confused, has not fixed it. Award the mark only for +543 with the sign explicitly stated.
