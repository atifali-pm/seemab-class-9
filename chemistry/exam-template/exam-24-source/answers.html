SECTION A: ANSWER KEY (10 × 1 = 10 MARKS)
1. Percentage of oxygen in clean, dry air: (b) 21% 1
Clean, dry air is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen; the remainder is noble gases and carbon dioxide. 78% is the trap: that is nitrogen.
Source: Ch 11, section 11.1 and Fig. 11.1, book p151. SLO: composition of clean, dry air.
2. Besides acid rain, sulphur dioxide is responsible for: (a) haze 1
The book: “Sulphur dioxide also responsible for acid rain and haze.” Melting of polar ice is an effect of global warming, caused by CO2 and methane, not SO2.
Source: Ch 11, section 11.2.1, book p152. SLO: adverse effects of air pollutants, (f) sulphur dioxide: acid rain and haze.
3. Particulate matter is harmful mainly because it can: (c) penetrate deep into the lungs, causing respiratory problems and lung cancer 1
Particulates are fine particles such as soot, smoke and dust. Option (d) describes carbon monoxide, which also comes from incomplete combustion; keep the two apart.
Source: Ch 11, section 11.2.4, book p153. SLO: (c) particulates, increased risk of respiratory problems and cancer.
4. Nearly half of all air pollution comes from: (d) cars and other motor vehicles 1
The book: “Nearly half of the air pollution comes from cars and other motor vehicles.” Volcanoes, forest fires, termites and cows are natural sources.
Source: Ch 11, section 11.3.2, book p154.
5. A catalytic converter contains: (b) platinum, palladium and rhodium 1
Calcium oxide is the trap: it is used in flue gas desulphurization in power plants, not in a car’s exhaust.
Source: Ch 11, section 11.6, book p158. SLO: removal of oxides of nitrogen by catalytic converters.
6. An important source of methane: (a) rice fields 1
Wetlands, rice fields, landfills and livestock are sources of methane. Car engines and lightning produce nitrogen oxides; coal-burning power stations produce sulphur dioxide.
Source: Ch 11, section 11.2.5, book p153. SLO: (c) methane from the decomposition of vegetation and waste gases from digestion in animals.
7. Photosynthesis removes from the air: (c) carbon dioxide 1
Photosynthesis: 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2. That is why planting trees is a strategy against global warming.
Source: Ch 11, sections 11.7 and 11.8, book p158–160. SLO: define photosynthesis.
8. [REVISION, Ch 10] Correct ionization of nitric acid: (b) HNO3 → H+ + NO3− 1
Ionization means the acid breaks up into ions, so the acid is on the left. Option (a) is written backwards, which is exactly the slip in exam-23 Q3(i). Strong acid, so a single arrow.
Re-test of exam-23 Q3(i) and (ii), both written in reverse. Ch 10 SLO: formulate dissociation equations.
9. [REVISION, Ch 10] Bronsted-Lowry acid in H2S + NH3 ⇌ NH4+ + HS−: (d) H2S 1
H2S donates a proton and becomes HS− (a negative ion). NH4+ is the trap: it is a product, formed when NH3 accepts the proton. Choose the acid from the left side of the arrow.
Source: Ch 10, Concept Assessment 10.1 Q3, book p144. Re-test of exam-23 Q2(ii), where the product H3O+ was named as the acid.
10. [REVISION, Ch 10] Magnesium burning in oxygen: (a) 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO 1
Oxygen gas is always O2. Option (b) balances only because it wrongly uses a single O atom.
Source: Ch 10, section 10.5, book p146. Re-test of exam-23 Q8(i), “2Mg + O → 2MgO”.
SECTION B: ANSWER KEY (6 × 4 = 24 MARKS)
2. Pollutants and their sources. 4
(i) “Anything that is in the air, water or soil which has a harmful effect on some part of the environment is called a pollutant.” 1
(ii) Any two, 1 mark each (source and its pollutant):
  • Forest fires and dust storms → smoke and dust particles
  • Volcanoes → clouds of dust, poisonous gases and ash
  • Termites and cows → methane
  • Electrical discharges in the atmosphere → nitrogen oxides
(iii) Burning fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas) in vehicles, factories or power plants → carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides and sulphur oxides. 1
Source: Ch 11, sections 11.2 (p152) and 11.3 (p154). SLO: state the major sources of air pollutants.
3. Formation of pollutants, balanced with state symbols. 4
(i)   2C(s) + O2(g) → 2CO(g)   (limited oxygen) 1
(ii)   S(s) + O2(g) → SO2(g) 1
(iii)   N2(g) + O2(g) → 2NO(g) 1
(iv)   2NO(g) + O2(g) → 2NO2(g) 1
Marking note: each mark is ½ for the balanced equation and ½ for state symbols. Her book prints (i) unbalanced as “C + O2 (limited) → CO”. The question asks for a balanced equation, so the 2 in front of C and CO is required; copying the book’s version loses the balancing ½. Source: Ch 11, section 11.3.2, book p154.
4. Carbon monoxide. 4
(i) By incomplete combustion, when there is not enough oxygen for the fuel to burn completely. 1
(ii) When inhaled, carbon monoxide combines with haemoglobin to form carboxyhaemoglobin 1, which cannot carry oxygen. The blood cells cannot absorb oxygen, so the person loses consciousness, suffocates and can die. 1
(iii) Carbon monoxide is a colourless and odourless gas. 1
Source: Ch 11, section 11.2.4, book p153. SLO: carbon monoxide from incomplete combustion; carbon monoxide as a toxic gas.
5. Global warming. 4
(i) Sunlight reaches the Earth and is absorbed by the surface, which then gives off energy as infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane absorb and re-radiate some of this infrared back, acting like a blanket, so less heat is lost to space 1. Human activities (burning fossil fuels, deforestation, livestock) increase these gases, so more heat is trapped and the Earth’s temperature rises: this is global warming. 1
(ii) Any two, 1 mark each:
  • The temperature of the Earth will gradually increase.
  • The climate may change, affecting rainfall: flooding in some regions and drought in others.
  • Polar ice may melt and cause a significant rise in sea levels.
  • The atmosphere becomes hotter.
Source: Ch 11, section 11.4 and Fig. 11.3, book p155–156. SLO: explain how CO2 and methane cause global warming.
6. Ground-level ozone. 4
(i) It is not released directly from any source; it is produced by reactions between other pollutants in sunlight. 1
(ii) In sunlight, nitrogen dioxide breaks up: NO2 → NO + O 1
    The atomic oxygen then joins an oxygen molecule: O + O2 → O3 1
(iii) Nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). 1 (½ each)
Source: Ch 11, section 11.2.6, book p153–154. SLO: ground-level ozone from reactions of oxides of nitrogen and VOCs in the presence of light. Note (ii): the atomic oxygen O reacts with the molecule O2; this is the one place in the chapter where a single O is correct.
7. [REVISION, Ch 8] Energy profile with and without a catalyst. 4
Solid black curve = without catalyst. Red dashed curve = with catalyst. Products sit lower than reactants (exothermic), so ΔH is negative.
(i) Marks: both axes labelled ½; reactants and products labelled, products lower ½; both humps, each with its Ea drawn from the reactant level up to the top of that hump 1; ΔH labelled between the reactant and product levels 1.
(ii) “A catalyst lowers the activation energy but does not change ΔH.” 1
Source: Ch 8, Fig. 8.1 (p130) and Fig. 8.3 with section 8.6 (p131): “The catalyst does not affect the overall thermodynamics or enthalpy of the reaction.” Re-test of exam-23 Q9, where ΔH and this sentence were both missing.
