SECTION A — Multiple Choice Questions Answer Key (12 × 1 = 12 Marks)
Q1. Anhydrous copper(II) sulphate turns _____ when it detects water.   ✓ B — blue 1 mark
Anhydrous copper(II) sulphate is a white crystalline solid. On absorbing water it becomes hydrated copper(II) sulphate, which is blue. This colour change from white to blue confirms the presence of water. Options A, C, and D describe colours that do not occur in this reaction.
Q2. The melting point of pure water is:   ✓ A — 0°C 1 mark
Pure water freezes at 0°C and boils at 100°C at sea level. These are standard reference values. Impurities lower the melting point below 0°C, which is how the purity test works. 4°C is the temperature of maximum density of water, not its melting point.
Q3. The pH of distilled water is:   ✓ C — 7 1 mark
Distilled water is free of all contaminants and has a neutral pH of 7. This is stated directly in Section 12.2.1. Tap water may have a slightly different pH because of dissolved minerals and treatment chemicals.
Q4. Substance added during the coagulation step:   ✓ B — slaked lime and alum 1 mark
In coagulation, slaked lime Ca(OH)2 and alum Al2(SO4)3 react to form a gelatinous mass of aluminium hydroxide Al(OH)3, which carries down dirt particles and bacteria. Chlorine is added in the later chlorination step, and sand/gravel are used in filtration.
Q5. Chlorination kills:   ✓ B — bacteria 1 mark
Chlorine reacts with water to form hypochlorous acid HClO, which is bactericidal. Heavy metals, phosphates, and nitrates are not removed by chlorination; they require physical or other chemical processes. Note: chlorination is not effective against viruses.
Q6. Waterborne disease caused by entamoeba:   ✓ C — Dysentery 1 mark
Dysentery is caused by the parasite entamoeba. It spreads through faecal contamination of water or food. Typhoid is caused by salmonella typhi bacteria, and hepatitis is caused by viruses. Jaundice is a symptom of liver disease, not a disease caused by entamoeba.
Q7. Which is NOT a waterborne disease?   ✓ D — Anaemia 1 mark
Anaemia is a blood condition caused by iron deficiency, not by contaminated water. The textbook lists dysentery, hepatitis, jaundice, and typhoid as waterborne diseases in Section 12.4. This question is drawn directly from Review Question 1(i).
Q8. High concentrations of phosphates cause:   ✓ B — Eutrophication 1 mark
Eutrophication is excessive algae growth in water bodies driven by phosphate and nitrate runoff from fertilizers and detergents. The algae deplete oxygen, killing aquatic life. Chlorination and sedimentation are water treatment steps, not consequences of phosphate pollution.
Q9. NPK fertilizers provide:   ✓ B — N, P, K 1 mark
NPK stands for nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K). These three elements are the main nutrients that fertilizers provide to enhance plant growth. Na (sodium) and Pb (lead) are not plant nutrients in this context; Ca (calcium) is a secondary nutrient, not one of the NPK trio.
Q10. Human activity that causes water contamination:   ✓ D — All of these 1 mark
Section 12.3 and Review Question 1(ii) confirm that livestock waste, pesticides, and septic tanks all contaminate water bodies. The correct answer is "all of these" because the textbook lists all three explicitly among sources of water pollution.
Q11. Distilled water is preferred in labs because:   ✓ B — it is free of contaminants 1 mark
Distilled water has undergone distillation to remove all dissolved solids and contaminants. Its electrical conductivity is very low (not high), and its pH is 7 (neutral). For laboratory reagents and calibration of analytical instruments, absence of contaminants is essential.
Q12. Water scarcity in Pakistan is mainly caused by:   ✓ B — population growth, climate change, and inadequate supply 1 mark
Section 12.6 states three causes: (1) rapid population growth increasing demand, (2) climate change causing irregular rainfall and declining aquifers, and (3) inadequate water supply from traditional flood irrigation leading to large evaporation losses. The other options are not causes of water scarcity.

2(i) Activity 12.1: Detection of water with anhydrous copper(II) sulphate 3 marks
Procedure: A small amount of anhydrous copper(II) sulphate powder (white) is placed in a dry test tube. A few drops of the water sample are added with a dropper. 1 mark
Colour change: The white powder turns light blue, confirming the presence of water. 1 mark
Equation: CuSO4 (white) + 5H2O → CuSO4·5H2O (blue). The product is hydrated copper(II) sulphate. 1 mark
OR
Melting point purity test: Ice cubes are made from the sample water and placed in a beaker. A thermometer is immersed and the ice is heated gradually. The temperature at which ice begins to melt is recorded. 1 mark
Standard value: Pure water melts at exactly 0°C. 1 mark
Interpretation: If the observed melting point is below 0°C, it indicates the presence of dissolved impurities (solutes depress the melting point), so the water is impure. 1 mark
2(ii) Properties of pure water / Impurities in natural water 3 marks
Physical properties of pure water:
  • Transparent, colourless, odourless, and tasteless. 1 mark
  • Boiling point: 100°C at sea level. 1 mark
  • Freezing (melting) point: 0°C at sea level. 1 mark
OR
Natural water is not pure because water is an excellent solvent. As rainwater falls through the atmosphere it dissolves: oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide, and dust particles. During thunderstorms it also dissolves nitric acid. 1 mark each for any four substances, up to 3 marks
Groundwater additionally dissolves minerals from rocks and soils and substances from decaying organisms.

Any three of the following, one mark each:
  • Plastics: harm aquatic life and disrupt ecosystems. 1 mark
  • Wastewater/sewage: carries bacteria, viruses, and pathogens that cause disease in humans and aquatic life. 1 mark
  • Metal compounds (toxic metals): excessive concentration is poisonous to aquatic organisms and humans. 1 mark
  • Nitrates from fertilizers: high concentrations in drinking water cause health risks, especially for young children. 1 mark
  • Phosphates from fertilizers/detergents: cause eutrophication, harmful algal blooms, and oxygen depletion. 1 mark
OR
Beneficial substances in natural water:
  • Dissolved oxygen: aquatic life depends on it for respiration; supports survival of fish and other organisms. 1 mark
  • Essential minerals (metal compounds): iron is needed for haemoglobin production; zinc, copper, and manganese activate enzyme activity for biological processes. 2 marks
2(iv) Steps of raw water treatment 3 marks
The four steps in order, one mark for any three correctly stated:
  1. Sedimentation: water stands in a reservoir; suspended matter sinks to the bottom. ½ mark
  2. Coagulation: slaked lime and alum are added; a gelatinous Al(OH)3 mass forms and carries down dirt and bacteria. ½ mark
  3. Filtration: water passes through sand and gravel (sometimes charcoal) to remove coloured and odorous compounds. ½ mark
  4. Chlorination: chlorine is added to kill remaining bacteria by forming HClO. ½ mark
(Award 1 mark per step correctly described, up to 3 marks.)
OR
Coagulation equation:
3Ca(OH)2(aq) + Al2(SO4)3(aq) → 2Al(OH)3(s) + 3CaSO4(aq) 2 marks
Product: Al(OH)3 is a gelatinous (jelly-like) mass of aluminium hydroxide. It traps and carries down fine dirt particles and bacteria as it settles, making the water clearer. 1 mark
2(v) Chlorination equation and mechanism 3 marks
Equation: Cl2(aq) + H2O(l) → HClO(aq) + H+(aq) + Cl−(aq) 1 mark
Acid produced: Hypochlorous acid (HClO). 1 mark
Mechanism: HClO is a powerful disinfectant; it kills any remaining bacteria in the water, making it safe to drink. This is the final step in the purification process. 1 mark
OR
Limitation of chlorination: Chlorination kills bacteria but is not effective against viruses such as the hepatitis virus. 1 mark
This is because viruses have a different structure from bacteria and HClO cannot destroy them at the concentrations used in water treatment. 1 mark
Alternative treatment: Viral contamination requires additional methods such as UV irradiation or boiling the water, which physically destroy virus particles. 1 mark

Water is heated and the temperature at which it boils is recorded. Pure water boils at exactly 100°C at sea level. 1 mark
A boiling point above 100°C indicates the presence of dissolved solids (solutes raise the boiling point). This is because dissolved impurities disrupt the escape of water molecules into vapour, requiring more energy (higher temperature). 1 mark

3(ii) Raw water treatment process / Chemical equations and public health 5 marks
(a) Four steps of raw water treatment [4 marks]:
  1. Sedimentation: Raw water is allowed to stand in a large reservoir. Suspended solid matter (mud, silt) sinks to the bottom due to gravity. No chemicals are added at this stage. 1 mark
  2. Coagulation: Water is treated with slaked lime Ca(OH)2 and alum Al2(SO4)3. They react to form a gelatinous mass of aluminium hydroxide Al(OH)3, which carries down fine dirt particles and bacteria as it settles. 1 mark
  3. Filtration: Water is filtered through beds of sand and gravel. Sometimes it is also filtered through activated charcoal to remove coloured and odorous compounds. 1 mark
  4. Chlorination: Chlorine gas is added in a controlled amount. It reacts with water to form hypochlorous acid HClO, which kills any remaining bacteria. 1 mark
(b) Flowchart [1 mark]:
Raw Water ⟶ Sedimentation ⟶ Coagulation ⟶ Filtration ⟶ Chlorination ⟶ Pure Water
OR
(a) Chemical equations [3 marks]:
Coagulation:
3Ca(OH)2(aq) + Al2(SO4)3(aq) → 2Al(OH)3(s) + 3CaSO4(aq)
The product Al(OH)3 (aluminium hydroxide) is the gelatinous mass that traps dirt and bacteria. 1½ marks

Chlorination:
Cl2(aq) + H2O(l) → HClO(aq) + H+(aq) + Cl−(aq)
The product HClO (hypochlorous acid) is the bactericidal agent. 1½ marks

(b) Water treatment and public health [2 marks]:
Water treatment is essential because untreated water contains bacteria, viruses, parasites, and heavy metals. 1 mark
Three diseases prevented: diarrhoea, dysentery, typhoid (or cholera, hepatitis, polio — any three from the textbook list accepted). 1 mark

3(iv) Water scarcity in Pakistan / NPK fertilizers and eutrophication 5 marks
(a) Causes and solutions of water scarcity in Pakistan [4 marks]:
Water scarcity is a major problem in Pakistan because demand exceeds the available safe water supply. 1 mark
Three causes:
  • Population growth: Pakistan's rapidly growing population increases water demand in agriculture, industry, and households. 1 mark
  • Climate change: irregular rainfall patterns and excessive groundwater extraction deplete aquifers; country's water resources are declining. 1 mark
  • Inadequate water supply: traditional flood irrigation causes significant losses through evaporation and poor water distribution. 1 mark
Three solutions: (i) effective water management practices; (ii) development of infrastructure; (iii) policy reforms + public awareness of responsible water use + strict regulation of groundwater drilling.

(b) Climate change and water scarcity [1 mark]:
Climate change causes irregular rainfall patterns, reducing water availability. The country's water resources decline because of excessive extraction from groundwater aquifers and insufficient irrigation practices. This means less water recharges underground reserves. 1 mark
OR
(a) NPK fertilizers [3 marks]:
Definition: Fertilizers are substances that provide essential elements for plant growth to enhance crop yields. 1 mark
NPK stands for nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K). These three main nutrients are provided by:
  • Nitrogen: urea, ammonium salts (e.g. ammonium nitrate), potassium nitrate. ½ mark
  • Phosphorus: di-ammonium phosphate, superphosphate. ½ mark
  • Potassium: potassium nitrate. ½ mark
Fertilizers dissolve in water, making nutrients available in a readily usable form for plants. ½ mark

(b) Excessive fertilizers and eutrophication [2 marks]:
When fertilizers are used in excess, nitrates and phosphates run off into nearby rivers, lakes, and water bodies through agricultural drainage. 1 mark
This causes eutrophication: phosphates trigger explosive growth of algae (algal blooms). The algae consume all available oxygen in the water. The resulting oxygen depletion kills fish and other aquatic organisms. 1 mark
