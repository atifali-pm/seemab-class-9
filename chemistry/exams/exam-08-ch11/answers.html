SECTION A — Multiple Choice Questions Answer Key (12 × 1 = 12 Marks)
Q1. The approximate percentage of nitrogen in clean, dry air is:   ✓ C — 78% 1 mark
Clean, dry air is approximately 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, and the remainder is noble gases and carbon dioxide. Option A (21%) is the percentage of oxygen, a common confusion. Argon accounts for approximately 0.93%.
Q2. Sulphur dioxide is produced mainly from:   ✓ B — burning of fossil fuels containing sulphur 1 mark
Sulphur is found naturally in fossil fuels. Burning these fuels in power plants, vehicles, and industrial units releases significant amounts of SO2. Decomposition of vegetation produces methane, not SO2. Photosynthesis consumes CO2 rather than producing pollutants.
Q3. Carbon monoxide is dangerous because it:   ✓ B — combines with haemoglobin to form carboxyhaemoglobin 1 mark
CO is colourless and odourless, so it cannot be detected by smell. It combines with haemoglobin to form carboxyhaemoglobin, which cannot carry oxygen, leading to suffocation and death. It does not cause acid rain — that is caused by SO2 and NOx.
Q4. NO2 is described as a:   ✓ B — reddish-brown gas with pungent odour 1 mark
The textbook states: NO2 is a reddish-brown gas with pungent odour soluble in water. NO (option A description) is colourless and odourless. SO2 is colourless with an irritating odour — a common distractor confusion.
Q5. Greenhouse gases act like a blanket by:   ✓ B — absorbing and re-radiating infrared radiation 1 mark
When the Earth's surface emits energy as infrared radiation, greenhouse gases absorb this energy and re-radiate some of it back towards Earth, trapping heat in the lower atmosphere. They do not reflect all sunlight back — visible light passes through. Filtering UV is the role of stratospheric ozone, a different topic.
Q6. Acid rain is defined as rain with a pH:   ✓ C — less than 5.6 1 mark
Acid rain is defined as rain having pH less than 5.6. Normal rainwater is saturated with CO2 and has a pH of 5.6. The pH of acid rain can be as low as 2.1, lower than vinegar or lemon juice. pH 7 is neutral, not acidic.
Q7. Ground-level ozone is classified as a:   ✓ B — secondary pollutant formed by reactions of NOx and VOCs in sunlight 1 mark
Ground-level ozone is a secondary pollutant; it is not emitted directly but forms when sunlight drives photochemical reactions between nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). It should not be confused with stratospheric ozone (option C describes stratospheric ozone's beneficial role). It is also not a significant greenhouse gas — that is option D's misleading framing.
Q8. The catalysts used in catalytic converters include:   ✓ B — platinum, palladium, and rhodium 1 mark
Catalytic converters use metals platinum (Pt), palladium (Pd), and rhodium (Rh) as catalysts. These are precious metals that facilitate conversion of CO and NOx into N2, CO2, and H2O. Iron and nickel are common industrial catalysts but not used in catalytic converters.
Q9. Which gas is primarily responsible for both acid rain AND global warming?   ✓ B — SO2 1 mark
SO2 causes acid rain (by forming H2SO4) and is listed in Table 11.1 as also contributing to the greenhouse effect. CO2 (option C) causes global warming but is not primarily responsible for acid rain. Ozone is a secondary pollutant. N2 is inert and causes neither.
Q10. Methane enters the atmosphere from livestock mainly through:   ✓ C — belching and flatulence during digestion 1 mark
The textbook states that animals such as cows, goats, and sheep have a unique digestive system that produces large amounts of methane, released through belching and flatulence. Wetlands, rice fields, and landfills are other methane sources, but combustion of fossil fuels (option D) does not specifically produce methane from livestock.
Q11. Which reaction correctly shows SO3 forming sulphuric acid?   ✓ B — SO3 + H2O → H2SO4 1 mark
The textbook equation is: SO3(g) + H2O(l) → H2SO4(aq). Option A uses H2 instead of H2O (incorrect). Option C uses O2 (incorrect reactant). Option D produces sulphurous acid H2SO3, which is formed from SO2 + H2O, not SO3.
Q12. Photosynthesis removes which pollutant from the atmosphere?   ✓ C — CO2 1 mark
Photosynthesis: 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2. Plants use carbon dioxide and water to produce glucose and oxygen. SO2, NO2, and CO are not substrates for photosynthesis; they are removed by other environmental or engineered processes.

2(i) OR — Atmosphere, water vapour, and variation 3 marks
The atmosphere is the envelope of gases and water vapour surrounding planet Earth. 1 Water vapour is gaseous water present in the air; it is not a fixed component. 1 The amount of water vapour varies because it depends on temperature and the availability of water bodies: over deserts it is very low, while in tropical rainforests it can reach up to 4% of air by volume. Temperature, altitude, and proximity to oceans all affect water vapour content. 1

A pollutant is anything in air, water, or soil that has a harmful effect on some part of the environment. 1 Four major air pollutants and their effects: 1
  • SO2: causes breathing difficulties, bronchitis, acid rain.
  • NOx: causes photochemical smog, acid rain, respiratory problems.
  • CO: combines with haemoglobin; causes brain damage and death.
  • CO2: increases global warming, contributing to climate change.

2(ii) OR — Natural vs human sources 3 marks
Natural sources are processes not caused by humans: 1 (i) forest fires release smoke and CO2; (ii) volcanic eruptions emit SO2, ash, and poisonous gases; (iii) electrical discharges (lightning) produce nitrogen oxides. 1 Human activity sources: (i) burning fossil fuels in vehicles and power plants releases SO2, NOx, CO; (ii) deforestation reduces CO2 absorption; (iii) incineration of waste produces smoke; (iv) industrial units release various pollutants. 1
2(iii) Properties and effects of SO2 3 marks
Physical properties of SO2: colourless gas with an unpleasant and irritating odour; soluble in water. 1 Sources: burning of fossil fuels in power stations and industries. 1 Harmful effects on human health: readily absorbed in the respiratory system; acts as a powerful irritant; aggravates symptoms of people suffering from asthma, bronchitis, and emphysema; also responsible for acid rain and haze. 1

2(iii) OR — Balanced equations for combustion reactions 3 marks
(a) C(s) + O2(g) [limited] → CO(g) [1 mark] (b) S(s) + O2(g) → SO2(g) [1 mark] (c) N2(g) + O2(g) → 2NO(g) [1 mark]
These reactions occur during the burning of fossil fuels; (a) shows incomplete combustion producing toxic CO; (b) shows sulphur combustion producing SO2; (c) shows nitrogen fixation in car engines at high temperature.
2(iv) Carbon monoxide: properties and toxicity 3 marks
Carbon monoxide (CO) is produced by incomplete combustion when there is not enough oxygen. 1 It is colourless and odourless, so it cannot be detected by the senses, making it extremely dangerous. 1 When inhaled, CO combines with haemoglobin to form carboxyhaemoglobin, which cannot carry oxygen. This prevents blood cells from absorbing oxygen, causing unconsciousness, suffocation, and ultimately death. 1
OR
2(iv) OR — Particulate matter 3 marks
Particulates (particulate matter) are fine particles suspended in the air, including soot, smoke, dust, and other solid or liquid substances. 1 Three examples: (i) soot from incomplete combustion; (ii) smoke from industrial units; (iii) dust from road surfaces and construction. 1 Harmful effects: particles that penetrate deep into the lungs cause respiratory problems and lung cancer; they also increase the risk of cardiovascular disease. 1
2(v) Greenhouse effect and greenhouse gases 3 marks
The greenhouse effect is the process by which greenhouse gases in the lower atmosphere trap heat energy, reducing thermal energy loss to space and helping to regulate the Earth's temperature. 1 Three greenhouse gases: water vapour (H2O), methane (CH4), and carbon dioxide (CO2). 1 These gases absorb infrared radiation emitted by the Earth's surface and re-radiate some of it back downward, acting like a blanket around the planet. Without this effect, the Earth would be much colder. 1
OR
2(v) OR — Global warming and its consequences 3 marks
Global warming is the warming of the atmosphere due to human influence on the greenhouse effect, caused by disturbing the natural balance of greenhouse gas concentrations. 1 Four consequences if global warming continues: 2 — award 0.5 per valid point
  • Temperature of the Earth will gradually increase.
  • Climate change will affect rates of rainfall, causing flooding in some regions and drought in others.
  • Polar ice will melt, causing a significant rise in sea levels.
  • The atmosphere becomes hotter overall.
2(vi) Methane: sources and warming role 3 marks
Methane (CH4) is a greenhouse gas that enters the atmosphere from a variety of sources. 1 Three sources: (i) decomposition of vegetation and waste in wetlands and landfills; (ii) rice fields; (iii) livestock (cows, goats, sheep) produce methane during digestion, released through belching and flatulence. 1 Higher levels of methane in the atmosphere contribute to global warming because methane absorbs and re-radiates infrared radiation, enhancing the greenhouse effect, which causes climate change. 1
OR
2(vi) OR — Ground-level ozone 3 marks
Ground-level ozone is often called smog. It is a secondary pollutant. 1 It is formed by complex photochemical reactions between nitrogen oxides (NOx) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) under the influence of sunlight. Sunlight breaks NO2 into NO and atomic oxygen; the atomic oxygen then reacts with O2 to form O3. 1 Harmful effects: (i) irritates the respiratory organs, causes asthma and respiratory problems; (ii) damages vegetation, reduces crop yield, and damages forests. 1
2(vii) Acid rain: definition, pH, and responsible acids 3 marks
Acid rain is defined as rain having a pH less than 5.6. 1 Normal rainwater is saturated with CO2 and has a pH of 5.6. Acid rain can have a pH as low as 2.1. 1 The two main acids responsible are sulphuric acid (H2SO4), formed from SO2 in the atmosphere, and nitric acid (HNO3), formed from NO2. 1
OR
2(vii) OR — Conversion of SO2 to sulphuric acid 3 marks
Step 1: Sulphur burns to form SO2 S(s) + O2(g) → SO2(g) [0.5 mark] Step 2: SO2 is oxidised to SO3 2SO2(g) + O2(g) → 2SO3(g) [1 mark] Step 3: SO3 dissolves in water to form H2SO4 SO3(g) + H2O(l) → H2SO4(aq) [1 mark] Also: 4NO2(g) + O2(g) + 2H2O(l) → 4HNO3(aq) [0.5 mark]
Nitrogen dioxide plays a catalytic role in the oxidation of SO2 to SO3 in the atmosphere, accelerating acid formation.

2(ix) Catalytic converters 3 marks
A catalytic converter is a device installed in the exhaust system of cars that converts harmful gases into less harmful substances. 1 The catalysts used are platinum (Pt), palladium (Pd), and rhodium (Rh). 1 The main conversion equation is: 1
2NO + 2HC/CO →(Pt/Pd)→ N2 + 2CO2 + H2O
The converter also transforms CO to CO2, NO to N2 and O2, and unburned hydrocarbons to CO2 and H2O.

2(ix) OR — Strategies to reduce acid rain 3 marks
Three strategies: 1 per strategy
  1. Emission control: reducing SO2 and NOx emissions from power plants, industries, and vehicles through anti-pollution technologies and cleaner fuels.
  2. Flue gas desulphurization: wet or dry scrubbers in power plants remove SO2 from flue gases before they are released into the atmosphere. Calcium oxide (CaO) is used to absorb SO2.
  3. Low sulphur fuels: promoting fuels with low sulphur content minimises the amount of sulphuric acid formed in the atmosphere.
2(x) Photosynthesis and its role in reducing CO2 3 marks
Photosynthesis is a biochemical process in which plants use solar energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose in the presence of chlorophyll. 1
6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2
1 mark for equation Photosynthesis removes CO2 from the atmosphere and turns it into organic matter, thereby maintaining the carbon cycle and helping to counteract the build-up of this greenhouse gas that drives global warming. Forests act as carbon sinks. 1

2(x) OR — Strategies to reduce global warming (climate change) 3 marks
Three strategies (1 mark each):
  1. Transition to renewable energy: increasing wind, solar, hydro, and geothermal energy reduces reliance on fossil fuels and cuts greenhouse gas emissions.
  2. Afforestation and reforestation: planting trees increases CO2 absorption through photosynthesis, reduces atmospheric CO2, and improves biodiversity.
  3. Reduce CO2 from transport: promoting electric vehicles and public transport reduces burning of fossil fuels, cutting CO2 and NOx emissions.
2(xi) Personal protective measures against air pollution 3 marks
(a) N95 masks are designed to filter out airborne particles including dust, smoke, and some airborne chemicals. They block at least 95% of particles, protecting the respiratory system from particulate matter and some chemical pollutants. 1.5 (b) Carbon monoxide detectors are installed in homes and offices to alert people to dangerous CO levels. Since CO is colourless and odourless, it cannot be detected by senses. The detector allows quick action: ventilating the area, sealing off sources, or evacuating. 1.5

2(xi) OR — Air quality index 3 marks
An air quality index (AQI) provides information about the general air quality of a location. 1 It uses a numerical scale or colour-coded system to indicate the level of air pollution, from clean (green) to hazardous (red/purple). 1 Practical uses: (i) a person with asthma can check the AQI before going outdoors and choose to stay indoors on high-pollution days; (ii) schools can cancel outdoor activities when the AQI reaches unhealthy levels, reducing children's exposure to ozone and particulates. 1

3(ii) Greenhouse effect and global warming in detail 5 marks
(a) Greenhouse effect explained: 3 When sunlight reaches the Earth, some is absorbed by the surface (vegetation, oceans, land) and warms it. As the surface heats up, it emits energy as infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases (water vapour, CO2, CH4) absorb this infrared energy. They then re-radiate some of it back towards the Earth's surface. This traps heat in the lower atmosphere, like a blanket. The process is a natural phenomenon of the Earth's energy distribution mechanism. Without it, the Earth would be too cold to support life (Fig. 11.3 in textbook). The higher the concentration of greenhouse gases, the greater the greenhouse effect and the greater the temperature increase. (b) Global warming is the warming of the atmosphere due to human influence on the greenhouse effect — specifically, disturbing the natural balance of greenhouse gas concentrations. 1 Human activities that intensify it: burning fossil fuels (increases CO2), deforestation (reduces CO2 absorption), industrial processes, and livestock farming (increases CH4). Three consequences: 1 gradual temperature rise; polar ice melting causing sea level rise; changing rainfall patterns causing flooding and drought.

3(iii) Acid rain: formation, chemistry, and effects 5 marks
(a) Acid rain is defined as rain having a pH less than 5.6. Normal rainwater has pH 5.6 due to dissolved CO2. Acid rain forms when SO2 and NO2 from burning fossil fuels dissolve in rainwater. 1 Full sequence for SO2 converting to H2SO4: 1
2SO2(g) + O2(g) → 2SO3(g) SO3(g) + H2O(l) → H2SO4(aq) [sulphuric acid]
Conversion of NO2 to HNO3: 1
4NO2(g) + O2(g) + 2H2O(l) → 4HNO3(aq) [nitric acid]
NO2 also acts catalytically in the oxidation of SO2 to SO3. (b) Four harmful effects: 2 — 0.5 per effect with equation
  • Marble/stone: CaCO3(s) + H2SO4(aq) → CaSO4(aq) + H2O + CO2 — statues and buildings disintegrate.
  • Iron metals: Fe(s) + H2SO4(aq) → FeSO4(aq) + H2(g) — metals corrode rapidly.
  • Aquatic life: lakes become too acidic for fish to survive; fish are killed and ecosystems destroyed.
  • Trees: acid rain leaches nutrients from soil and directly damages leaves and bark, destroying forests.

3(iii) OR — Strategies to solve acid rain and catalytic converters 5 marks
(a) Three strategies: 3
  1. Emission control: reducing SO2 and NOx from industrial units, power plants, and vehicles using anti-pollution technologies and cleaner fuels. This directly reduces the precursors of acid rain.
  2. Flue gas desulphurization: wet or dry scrubbers in power plants remove SO2 from flue gases before atmospheric release. Calcium oxide (CaO) absorbs SO2: CaO + SO2 → CaSO3.
  3. Low sulphur fuels: promoting use of fuels with low sulphur content minimises SO2 formation and consequently reduces sulphuric acid formation in the atmosphere.
(b) Catalytic converters are installed in car exhaust systems. They convert NO and CO (acid rain and global warming precursors) into N2, CO2, and H2O: 2
2NO + 2HC/CO →(Pt/Pd)→ N2 + 2CO2 + H2O
Catalysts: platinum, palladium, and rhodium. They are effective because they lower the activation energy for these reactions without being consumed. This prevents NO from forming HNO3 (a component of acid rain).

6CO2(g) + 6H2O(l) → C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(g)
1 mark for equation Photosynthesis plays a key role in the carbon cycle by removing CO2 from the atmosphere and converting it into organic matter. Forests act as major carbon sinks. Deforestation therefore increases atmospheric CO2, while afforestation reduces it. This makes photosynthesis an essential natural counterbalance to fossil fuel combustion. 1 (b) Three risk factors and linked diseases: 2

  • Indoor pollution: poor ventilation, mould, dust, mites, and household chemicals cause respiratory problems, allergies, and asthma.

3(iv) OR — Catalytic converters and personal protective measures 5 marks
(a) Catalytic converters are installed in the exhaust system of cars to reduce harmful emissions. 1 Three catalysts used: platinum (Pt), palladium (Pd), and rhodium (Rh). 1 Main conversion:
