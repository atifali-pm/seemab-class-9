💧 Hissa 2: Arrhenius concept

10.1.1 Arrhenius Concept of Acids and Bases (kitab p141, 142)

1887 mein Sweden ke chemist Svante Arrhenius ne acids aur bases ki pehli kamyab theory di. Ye theory paani ke baare mein hai: cheez ko paani mein daalo aur dekho kya nikalta hai.

📖 Kitab ki definition: exam mein yehi likho An acid is a substance that ionizes in water to produce H⁺ ions and a base is a substance that ionizes in water to produce OH⁻ ions.
🍪 Ionize ka matlab: biscuit todna Ionize = molecule ka tootna, ions mein. Jaise biscuit todo: poora biscuit pehle (left side), tukre baad mein (right side).
🎯 Pakki trick: equation ULTI mat likhna Pichle paper mein equation ulti likh di thi ("H + NO₃ → HNO₃"). Hamesha yaad rakho:
Poora molecule LEFT par → toote hue ions RIGHT par.
Sahi: HNO₃ → H⁺ + NO₃⁻    Ghalat: H⁺ + NO₃⁻ → HNO₃

Kitab ki equations (arrow ke upar H₂O ka matlab: paani mein):

CheezPaani mein tootne ki equation
HCl (acid)HCl(g) → H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)
HNO₃ (acid)HNO₃(l) → H⁺(aq) + NO₃⁻(aq)
H₂SO₄ (acid)H₂SO₄(l) → 2H⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq)
NaOH (base)NaOH(s) → Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)

H₂SO₄ mein 2 H hain, is liye 2H⁺ nikalte hain. Acid se hamesha H⁺, base se hamesha OH⁻.

🍫 Hissa 3: Bronsted-Lowry concept

10.1.2 The Bronsted-Lowery Concept of Acids and Bases (kitab p142, 143)

Pehle: Arrhenius theory ki kami

Arrhenius theory sirf paani wale solutions (aqueous) par chalti hai. Aur ye nahin bata sakti ke:

  • CO₂ aur SO₂ acidic kyun hain? Un mein to koi H hi nahin!
  • NH₃ base kyun hai? Us mein to koi OH hi nahin!

Is kami ko door karne ke liye 1923 mein J. N. Bronsted aur T. M. Lowry ne alag alag (independently) nayi theory di.

📖 Kitab ki definition: exam mein yehi likho According to this theory, an acid is a proton donor and a base is a proton acceptor.

Kitab ki pehli misal (p142, dot diagram):

HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻
   ..          ..                 [    H    ]⁺       ..  ⁻
 H─Cl:   +   H─O:     ──►        [    |    ]      [ :Cl: ]
   ''          |                  [ H─O:─H  ]      [  ''  ]
 Bronsted      H                    Hydronium ion   Chloride ion
 Acid       Bronsted Base

Kitab ki tasveer (dot diagram) mein: HCl ka H gol daire mein hai aur ek mura hua arrow (curved arrow) dikhata hai ke ye H⁺ paani ke O ki taraf jata hai. Paani ka O apne akele electron pair (lone pair) se ye proton pakar leta hai. Is tarah [H₃O]⁺ bracket mein + charge ke saath banta hai (O teen H se juda, ek lone pair baqi), aur Cl⁻ char lone pairs ke saath − charge par reh jata hai.

  • HCl apna H⁺ (proton) paani ko de deta hai: HCl = Bronsted acid.
  • Paani proton le leta hai: H₂O = Bronsted base.
  • Banne wali cheezein: H₃O⁺ = hydronium ion, Cl⁻ = chloride ion. (Hydronium ka naam yaad rakho!)
❓ Kitab ke 4 sawal is misal par (p143) Q1. Which substance is donating proton? HCl
Q2. Which substance is accepting proton? H₂O
Q3. Which substance is an acid? HCl
Q4. Which substance is a base? H₂O
🍫 Chocolate wali misal (sab se aasan) Proton = H⁺ = ek chocolate.
Jo chocolate DETA hai (donor) woh ACID.
Jo chocolate LETA hai (acceptor) woh BASE.
Bas itni si baat hai!
🎯 Pakki trick: H gino! Pichle paper mein kabhi paani ka role ulta ho gaya, kabhi H₃O⁺ ko acid likh diya. Ab ye 3 steps karo:
1. Reaction ka acid aur base hamesha LEFT side (reactants) mein se chuno. Right side wali cheezein (H₃O⁺, Cl⁻, NH₄⁺, OH⁻) banne wali cheezein (products) hain, woh is reaction ka acid ya base nahin. (Khud woh bhi proton de ya le sakti hain: H₃O⁺ aur NH₄⁺ proton de sakte hain, OH⁻ aur Cl⁻ le sakte hain. Lekin sawal hamesha reaction ke acid/base ka hota hai.)
2. Left wali cheez ka right side par kya bana? H kam hua (chocolate de di) → woh ACID.
3. H barh gaya (chocolate mil gayi) → woh BASE.
✏️ Example 10.1 (kitab p143): "Classify substances as acids or bases or as proton donor or proton acceptor" (a) HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻
HCl → Cl⁻H kam hua, chocolate diHCl = acid
H₂O → H₃O⁺H barha, chocolate liH₂O = base
(b) H₂O + NH₃ ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻
H₂O → OH⁻H kam hua, chocolate diH₂O = acid
NH₃ → NH₄⁺H barha, chocolate liNH₃ = base
Kitab ka solution: 1. Because HCl is converted to Cl⁻ by donating proton, HCl is an acid. 2. Because H₂O accepts the proton that HCl donates and forms H₃O⁺, water is a base. 3. H₂O is converted to OH⁻ by donating a proton, so H₂O is an acid. Because NH₃ accepts the proton and forms NH₄⁺, so it is a base.

Ammonia wali misal (kitab p143): Arrhenius theory nahin bata sakti ke ammonia (NH₃) base kaise hai, kyunke us mein OH hai hi nahin. Bronsted-Lowry theory batati hai. Ammonia room temperature par gas hai. Paani mein ghulne par:

NH₃ + H₂O → NH₄⁺ + OH⁻
      ..                         [    H    ]⁺     [ ..    ]⁻
   H─N─H   +   H─O:   ──►        [ H─N─H   ]      [ :O─H  ]
      |            |             [    H    ]      [ ''    ]
      H            H              Ammonium ion    Hydroxide ion

Kitab ki tasveer mein: NH₃ ke N par ek lone pair hai. Mura hua arrow N ke lone pair se paani ke gol daire wale H ki taraf jata hai: NH₃ paani ka H⁺ le leta hai. Is se [NH₄]⁺ (bracket, + charge) aur [:O─H]⁻ (bracket, − charge) bante hain.

Paani apna ek H⁺ NH₃ ko deta hai. NH₃ ban jata hai NH₄⁺ (ammonium ion) aur paani ban jata hai OH⁻ (hydroxide ion). Is liye yahan NH₃ = base (proton acceptor) aur H₂O = acid (proton donor).

⭐ Kitab ki do lines (p143) "All the acids included in the Arrhenius theory are also acids in the Bronsted-Lowry theory."
"However, all the bases included in Bronsted-Lowry theory except OH⁻ are not Arrhenius bases."
Matlab: har Arrhenius acid Bronsted acid bhi hai. Lekin Bronsted bases (jaise NH₃) Arrhenius bases nahin, kyunke un mein OH nahin hota. Sirf OH⁻ dono theories mein base hai: Arrhenius base OH⁻ deta hai, aur OH⁻ khud proton le leta hai (Bronsted base).

Dekha? Paani (a) mein base tha aur (b) mein acid! Isi liye kitab kehti hai: water behaves like an acid as well as a base. It is amphoteric in nature.

📖 Kitab ki definition Substances that react with both acids and bases are called amphoteric substances.
👭 Paani ki misal Paani us saheli jaisa hai jo HCl ke saath ho to chocolate le leti hai (base), aur NH₃ ke saath ho to chocolate de deti hai (acid). Saathi dekh kar role badalta hai!
⭐ Kitab ki problem solving strategy (p143) 1. Acid proton de kar negative ion ban jata hai (HCl → Cl⁻).
2. Base proton le kar positive ion ban jata hai (NH₃ → NH₄⁺, H₂O → H₃O⁺).
Aur yaad rakho: Arrhenius wale sab acids Bronsted-Lowry mein bhi acids hain.

⚗️ Hissa 5: Acids aur bases ke kaam

10.3 Characteristic properties of acids (kitab p145, 146)

Acids ki 5 pehchan:

  1. Acids have sour taste.
  2. Acids change the colour of blue litmus paper to red.
  3. Metals ke saath: "Acids react with most metals and corrode them." Zinc, magnesium, aluminium aur calcium jaise metals ke saath un ka salt aur hydrogen gas banti hai, jo bulbulon ki shakal mein nikalti hai. Zinc + hydrochloric acid → zinc chloride + hydrogen. Magnesium + sulphuric acid → magnesium sulphate + hydrogen.
  4. Metal carbonates ke saath: salt + water + carbon dioxide, jo paani mein bulbule banati hai. Hydrochloric acid + sodium carbonate → sodium chloride + carbon dioxide + water. Sulphuric acid + calcium carbonate (limestone ya marble) → calcium sulphate + carbon dioxide + water.
  5. Bases ke saath: salt + water. Isay neutralization reaction kehte hain. Hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water. Sulphuric acid + potassium hydroxide → potassium sulphate + water.
📖 Kitab ke alfaaz (p145): acids ki properties 3, 4, 5 3. Acids react with most metals and corrode them. Acids combine with metals like zinc, magnesium, aluminium and calcium to form their salts and hydrogen gas. The hydrogen gas is liberated in the form of bubbles. Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen gas
4. Acids react with metal carbonates to form salts, water with the liberation of carbon dioxide. The liberated carbon dioxide forms bubbles in water. Metal carbonate + Acid → Salt + Water + Carbon dioxide. This reaction is used in the industrial preparation of glass, paper and soap.
5. Acids combine with bases to produce salt and water. This reaction is called as neutralization reaction. Acid + Base → Salt + Water
🧠 Teen reactions, ek trick: "Salt har jagah!" Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen
Acid + Carbonate → Salt + Water + CO₂  (Carbonate se CO₂)
Acid + Base → Salt + Water  (neutralization)

Acid rain (kitab p145, 146):

📖 Kitab ke alfaaz: exam mein yehi likho (SLO: Define acid rain, effects of acid rain) Normal rain is slightly acidic due to dissolved carbon dioxide. As a result of human activity, many oxides enter the atmosphere, which makes rainwater more acidic, which falls as acid rain. Acid rain contains dissolved nitric and sulphuric acid.   • Acids corrosive hain, is liye acid rain metals aur metal carbonates wali buildings, structures aur statues ko nuqsan deti hai. (Is ki poori kahani Chapter 11 mein hai.)

  • Misalein: aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃), aluminium hydroxide (Al(OH)₃), zinc oxide (ZnO), zinc hydroxide (Zn(OH)₂).
📖 Kitab ke alfaaz (p146): exam mein yehi likhoSome metal oxides and hydroxides do not dissolve in water but behave in chemical reactions like both acids and bases. Such metal oxides and hydroxides are called amphoteric oxides and hydroxides, respectively.
🧠 Amphoteric ki trick: "A se Z tak" Aluminium aur Zinc: A se Z, yani dono taraf! In ke oxides aur hydroxides acid aur base dono ke saath react karte hain.
✨ Ek line mein Metal + O₂ → metal oxide; metal oxide + paani → metal hydroxide (base). Aluminium aur zinc wale = amphoteric.

📝 Key Points: kitab ke alfaaz mein

  • According to Arrhenius theory, an acid is a substance that ionizes in water to produce H⁺ ions and a base is a substance that ionizes in water to produce OH⁻ ions.
  • A Bronsted-Lowry acid is a proton donor and a base is a proton acceptor.
  • A strong acid completely dissociates in aqueous solution and a weak acid dissociates partially in aqueous solution.
  • A strong base completely dissociates in aqueous solution and a weak base dissociates partially in aqueous solution.
  • Bases are oxides and hydroxides of metals.
  • Alkalis are water soluble bases.
  • Acids neutralize bases to form salt and water.
  • Acids decompose carbonates to form salt, water, and carbon dioxide.
  • Acids corrode metals and form salt and hydrogen.

Q2. Give short answer

  1. Write the equation for the self-ionization of water.
    H₂O + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ (ek paani molecule proton deta hai, doosra leta hai; paani amphoteric hai)
  2. Define and give examples of Arrhenius acids.
    An Arrhenius acid is a substance that ionizes in water to produce H⁺ ions. Examples: HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄, H₃PO₄.
  3. Why HCl acts as a strong acid?
    Because it ionizes completely (100%) in aqueous solution: HCl(g) → H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq).
  4. Why NH₃ acts as Bronsted-Lowry base?
    Because it accepts a proton from water and forms NH₄⁺: NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻.
  5. Why ammonia acts as a weak base?
    Because it ionizes only to a little extent in water and produces fewer OH⁻ ions: H₂O + NH₃ ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻.
