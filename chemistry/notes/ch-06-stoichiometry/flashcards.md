## Topic 1 — Empirical vs Molecular Formula

### Easy
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What is an empirical formula? | The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound. |
| What is a molecular formula? | The actual number of each type of atom in one molecule of a compound. |
| What does "empirical" mean in the phrase "empirical formula"? | Based on experiment or experience. |
| Give one example of a compound where molecular and empirical formulas are the same. | H₂O (also CO₂, NH₃, CH₄). |
| Molecular formula of glucose? | C₆H₁₂O₆ |

### Medium
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Empirical formula of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)? | CH₂O |
| Empirical formula of benzene (C₆H₆)? | CH |
| Empirical formula of hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)? | HO |
| Empirical formula of ethene (C₂H₄)? | CH₂ |
| Compound has 2 C, 4 H, 2 O. What is its empirical formula? | CH₂O |

### Hard
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| How do you find an empirical formula from a molecular formula? | Divide all subscripts by their highest common factor (HCF). |
| If a compound has molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₆ and empirical formula CH₂O, what must its molecular mass be a multiple of? | 30 (the mass of the empirical unit). |
| Two compounds have the same empirical formula (CH₂O). What's the simplest way they could still be different compounds? | Different molecular masses — one might be CH₂O, another could be C₂H₄O₂ (acetic acid) or C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose). |

## Topic 2 — Molecular Mass & Formula Mass

### Easy
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What is molecular mass? | The sum of atomic masses of all atoms in one molecule. |
| Unit of molecular mass? | amu (atomic mass unit). |
| What is formula mass? | The sum of atomic masses of all atoms in one formula unit of an ionic compound. |
| Which term is used for ionic compounds: molecular mass or formula mass? | Formula mass. |
| Atomic mass of hydrogen? | 1 amu (1.008 amu exactly). |
| Atomic mass of oxygen? | 16 amu |
| Atomic mass of carbon? | 12 amu |
| Atomic mass of sodium? | 23 amu |
| Atomic mass of chlorine? | 35.5 amu |

### Medium
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Molecular mass of H₂O? | 18 amu |
| Molecular mass of CO₂? | 44 amu |
| Molecular mass of NH₃? | 17 amu |
| Molecular mass of CH₄? | 16 amu |
| Formula mass of NaCl? | 58.5 amu |
| Formula mass of CaCO₃? | 100 amu |
| Formula mass of Mg(OH)₂? | 58 amu |
| Formula mass of H₂SO₄? | 98 amu |
| Formula mass of KClO₃? | 122.5 amu |
| Formula mass of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)? | 180 amu |

### Hard
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Formula mass of CuSO₄·5H₂O (Cu=63.5, S=32, O=16, H=1)? | 249.5 amu |
| A compound Na₂CX₃ has formula mass 106 amu. What is the atomic mass of X? | 12 (it's sodium carbonate Na₂CO₃). |
| Why are molecular and formula mass calculated the same way even though they have different names? | Because both are just "sum of atomic masses of every atom in the formula" — the calculation doesn't change. The name just describes whether the compound is molecular or ionic. |

## Topic 3 — The Mole & Avogadro's Number

### Easy
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What is a mole? | The amount of a substance that contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles. |
| What is Avogadro's number? | 6.022 × 10²³ |
| Symbol for Avogadro's number? | N_A |
| Who is Avogadro's number named after? | Amedeo Avogadro, an Italian chemist. |
| 1 dozen = ? | 12 |
| 1 mole = ? particles | 6.022 × 10²³ |

### Medium
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| How many carbon atoms in 1 mole of carbon? | 6.022 × 10²³ |
| How many water molecules in 1 mole of water? | 6.022 × 10²³ |
| How many molecules in 2 moles of CO₂? | 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.2044 × 10²⁴ |
| How many molecules in 0.5 moles of O₂? | 0.5 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.011 × 10²³ |
| How many atoms in 0.25 moles of Na? | 0.25 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.506 × 10²³ |

### Hard
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| A compound has 3.011 × 10²³ molecules. How many moles is that? | 0.5 moles (it's half of Avogadro's number). |
| Why did chemists invent the mole instead of just counting atoms? | Atoms are too small to count individually. The mole is a convenient counting unit that makes the numbers practical. |
| If one mole of a substance contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles, how many particles in 0.001 mole? | 6.022 × 10²⁰ |

## Topic 4 — Molar Mass

### Easy
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What is molar mass? | The mass of one mole of a substance, in grams. |
| What is gram atomic mass? | The atomic mass of an element expressed in grams (= 1 mole of that element). |
| What is gram molecular mass? | The molecular mass of a compound expressed in grams. |
| What is gram formula mass? | The formula mass of an ionic compound expressed in grams. |
| What is the formula for moles from mass? | n = mass ÷ molar mass |
| Mass of 1 mole of carbon? | 12 g |
| Mass of 1 mole of water? | 18 g |
| Mass of 1 mole of NaCl? | 58.5 g |

### Medium
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| How many moles in 36 g of water? | 36 ÷ 18 = 2 moles |
| How many moles in 44 g of CO₂? | 44 ÷ 44 = 1 mole |
| How many moles in 11 g of CO₂? | 11 ÷ 44 = 0.25 moles |
| Mass of 0.5 moles of NaCl? | 0.5 × 58.5 = 29.25 g |
| Mass of 2 moles of water? | 2 × 18 = 36 g |
| Mass of 0.25 moles of CO₂? | 0.25 × 44 = 11 g |
| Mass of 1.5 moles of H₂SO₄? | 1.5 × 98 = 147 g |

### Hard
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| How many molecules are present in 0.25 moles of SO₂? | 0.25 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.5055 × 10²³ molecules |
| How many moles are present in 1 billion (10⁹) atoms of magnesium? | 10⁹ ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.66 × 10⁻¹⁵ moles |
| How many atoms in 1.25 moles of Zn? | 1.25 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 7.53 × 10²³ atoms |
| Why is the molar mass of water (18 g) numerically the same as its molecular mass (18 amu)? | By design — the mole was defined so that atomic mass in amu equals molar mass in grams. This is the bridge between the atomic and macroscopic worlds. |

## Topic 5 — Binary Ionic Formulas

### Easy
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What is a cation? | A positively charged ion (usually a metal). |
| What is an anion? | A negatively charged ion (usually a non-metal). |
| What is the charge of sodium ion? | +1 (Na⁺) |
| What is the charge of chloride ion? | −1 (Cl⁻) |
| What is the charge of calcium ion? | +2 (Ca²⁺) |
| What is the charge of oxide ion? | −2 (O²⁻) |
| What is the charge of aluminium ion? | +3 (Al³⁺) |

### Medium
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Formula of sodium chloride? | NaCl |
| Formula of potassium oxide? | K₂O |
| Formula of magnesium chloride? | MgCl₂ |
| Formula of calcium oxide? | CaO |
| Formula of aluminium oxide? | Al₂O₃ |
| Formula of aluminium chloride? | AlCl₃ |
| Formula of magnesium nitride (Mg²⁺ and N³⁻)? | Mg₃N₂ |

### Hard
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Why must an ionic compound be electrically neutral? | Because the total positive charge from cations must equal the total negative charge from anions; otherwise the compound would have a net charge. |
| What's the crisscross method? | Use the charge of one ion as the subscript of the other. For Al³⁺ and O²⁻, you get Al₂O₃. |
| Formula of ammonium sulfate (NH₄⁺ and SO₄²⁻)? | (NH₄)₂SO₄ |

## Topic 6 — Balancing Chemical Equations

### Easy
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What is a reactant? | A substance that starts a chemical reaction (on the left of the arrow). |
| What is a product? | A substance formed in a chemical reaction (on the right of the arrow). |
| What does the arrow in a chemical equation mean? | "Becomes" or "reacts to form". |
| State symbol for solid? | (s) |
| State symbol for liquid? | (l) |
| State symbol for gas? | (g) |
| State symbol for aqueous (dissolved in water)? | (aq) |

### Medium
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| In balancing, what can you change? | The coefficients (big numbers in front of formulas). |
| In balancing, what can you NEVER change? | The subscripts inside a chemical formula. |
| Why can't you change subscripts? | Changing a subscript changes the identity of the compound (e.g., H₂O → H₂O₂ is a completely different substance). |
| Balance: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O | 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O |
| Balance: Na + Cl₂ → NaCl | 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl |
| Balance: Mg + O₂ → MgO | 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO |
| Balance: CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |

### Hard
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Balance: Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃ | 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃ |
| Balance: Al + HCl → AlCl₃ + H₂ | 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂ |
| Balance: C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O (propane combustion) | C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| What law of physics requires us to balance equations? | The Law of Conservation of Mass — matter is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. |
| Who discovered that law? | Antoine Lavoisier in the 18th century. |

## Topic 7 — Ionic Equations

### Easy
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What is an ionic equation? | A chemical equation in which aqueous compounds are written as their separated ions. |
| What is a net ionic equation? | An ionic equation with the spectator ions removed. |
| What are spectator ions? | Ions that appear unchanged on both sides of an ionic equation and don't participate in the reaction. |
| Which compounds do you split into ions in an ionic equation? | Only aqueous (aq) compounds. |
| Which compounds do you NOT split? | Solids (s), liquids (l), and gases (g). |

### Medium
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Net ionic equation for: HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)? | H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O |
| Net ionic equation for: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq)? | Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl(s) |
| In the neutralization equation above, what are the spectator ions? | Na⁺ and Cl⁻ |

### Hard
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Why do different acid-base neutralizations have the same net ionic equation? | Because the underlying reaction is always H⁺ combining with OH⁻ to form water. The other ions are spectators. |
| Who first proposed the theory of ionic dissociation (that salts break into ions in water)? | Svante Arrhenius in 1884. His PhD committee almost failed him for the idea — he won the Nobel Prize for it in 1903. |

## Topic 8 — Molecular vs Structural Formula

### Easy
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What does a molecular formula tell you? | The number of each type of atom in a molecule. |
| What does a structural formula show? | How the atoms are connected to each other. |
| Molecular formula of n-butane? | C₄H₁₀ |
| Structural formula of n-butane? | CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃ |

### Medium
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| Molecular formula of CH₃–CH₂–OH? | C₂H₆O |
| Molecular formula of CH₃–CO–CH₃? | C₃H₆O |
| Molecular formula of CH₃–CH₂–NH₂? | C₂H₇N |

### Hard
| Q | A |
|---|---|
| What are isomers? | Compounds that have the same molecular formula but different structural formulas. |
| Give an example of two compounds with the same molecular formula C₂H₆O. | Ethanol (CH₃–CH₂–OH) and dimethyl ether (CH₃–O–CH₃). |
| Why does the structural formula matter if the molecular formula is the same? | Because different arrangements give different physical and chemical properties — e.g., ethanol is a liquid you can drink, while dimethyl ether is a dangerous gas. |
