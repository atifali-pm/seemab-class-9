SECTION A — MCQ Answer Key (12 × 1 = 12 Marks)
1
Answer: B — 60 1 mark
By definition, 1° = 60′ (minutes). There are 3600 seconds in a degree, not minutes. Distractor D (3600) is the seconds conversion, a common mix-up.
2
Answer: B — 2π radians 1 mark
The circumference-to-radius ratio of a full circle is 2π, so a complete rotation = 2π radians. π radians corresponds to only a half circle (180°).
3
Answer: C — l = rθ 1 mark
Arc length = radius × central angle in radians. This follows from the proportionality l : θ :: r : 1, giving l = rθ. θ must be in radians.
4
Answer: B — 1/2 1 mark
In a 30-60-90 triangle, the side opposite 30° is half the hypotenuse, so sin 30° = 1/2. Option A (√3/2) is sin 60°, a frequent confusion.
5
Answer: C — sinθ 1 mark
In the second quadrant x<0, y>0. Since sinθ = y/r with r always positive, sinθ>0. cosθ<0 (x<0) and tanθ<0 (y/x = positive/negative).
6
Answer: C — sin2θ + cos2θ = 1 1 mark
The fundamental Pythagorean identity. Dividing a2+b2=c2 by c2 gives this. Option B should be sec2θ − tan2θ = 1 (not plus), making it wrong.
7
Answer: B — Base / Hyp 1 mark
cosθ = Base/Hypotenuse = b/c. The base is the side adjacent to angle θ and the hypotenuse is the longest side.
8
Answer: D — 50.5° 1 mark
50°30′ = 50° + (30/60)° = 50° + 0.5° = 50.5°. This is a direct application of D°M′ to decimal conversion from the Miscellaneous Exercise.
9
Answer: B — Angle of elevation 1 mark
Angle of elevation is formed when the object is above the horizontal line of sight. Angle of depression applies when the object is below.
10
Answer: C — North 1 mark
Bearings are measured clockwise from north and expressed as three-figure numbers (000° to 360°). North itself has bearing 000°.
11
Answer: C — 090° 1 mark
From the Key Points: north = 000°, east = 090°, south = 180°, west = 270°. East is a quarter-turn clockwise from north.
12
Answer: B — 45° 1 mark
cos x = sin x implies tan x = 1, so x = 45°. At 45° the right triangle is isosceles (base = perpendicular), making sin and cos equal (both = 1/√2). From Miscellaneous Exercise Q(v).

3(ii) — Prove two trigonometric identities 5 marks
Identity (a): (sinθ − 2sin³θ) / (2cos³θ − cosθ) = tanθ 2 marks
L.H.S. = sinθ(1 − 2sin²θ) / cosθ(2cos²θ − 1)
Use: 1 − 2sin²θ = 2cos²θ − 1 (both equal cos 2θ form; or note they are equal via sin²+cos²=1)
= sinθ(1 − 2sin²θ) / cosθ(1 − 2sin²θ) = sinθ/cosθ = tanθ = R.H.S. Hence proved.
Identity (b): √(sec²θ + cosec²θ) = tanθ + cotθ 3 marks
R.H.S. = tanθ + cotθ = sinθ/cosθ + cosθ/sinθ = (sin²θ + cos²θ)/(sinθcosθ) = 1/(sinθcosθ)
L.H.S. = √(1/cos²θ + 1/sin²θ) = √((sin²θ+cos²θ)/(sin²θcos²θ)) = √(1/(sin²θcos²θ)) = 1/(sinθcosθ)
L.H.S. = R.H.S. — Hence proved.
OR — Prove two OR identities
Identity (a): (1−sinθ)/(1+sinθ) = (secθ−tanθ)2 2 marks
R.H.S. = (1/cosθ − sinθ/cosθ)2 = ((1−sinθ)/cosθ)2 = (1−sinθ)2/cos²θ
= (1−sinθ)2/(1−sin²θ) = (1−sinθ)2/((1−sinθ)(1+sinθ)) = (1−sinθ)/(1+sinθ) = L.H.S.
Identity (b): sin6θ + cos6θ = 1 − 3sin²θcos²θ 3 marks
Use a³+b³ = (a+b)(a²−ab+b²) with a=sin²θ, b=cos²θ:
= (sin²θ+cos²θ)(sin4θ−sin²θcos²θ+cos4θ)
= 1 × ((sin²θ+cos²θ)2 − 3sin²θcos²θ) = 1 − 3sin²θcos²θ = R.H.S. Hence proved.
