🗺️ 1. Application of Venn diagrams (p57, p58)

📖 Kitab ka jumla Venn diagram is a practical mathematical tool for solving real world problems of set theory.

Lafzon wala sawal aaye to seedha formula mat lagao, pehle naqsha banao. Aur naqsha hamesha andar se bahar ki taraf bharo: sab se pehle beech wala hissa (jo teeno mein aata hai), phir do-do ke hisse, phir akele wale, aur aakhir mein rectangle ke bahar ka number. Jo log do cheezon mein aate hain woh donon circles mein gine ja chuke hote hain, is liye seedha jorne se jawab hamesha zyada aata hai.

✏️ Example 11 (p57), subset wala case Among the ten teachers of a secondary school, five teach Mathematics and three teach Biology. However, all these teachers also teach English. Show the data by Venn diagram. Also find how many teachers teach only English.
Solution. E = set of English Teachers, M = set of Mathematics Teachers, B = set of Biology Teachers. Both M and B are the subsets of E, so dono chhote ovals bare oval E ke andar banenge.
Only English = 10 − 5 − 3 = 2 teachers.

Saray saat hisse joro to n(C ∪ H ∪ F) = 34+2+3+5+6+26+20 = 96, aur n(U) = 96 + 12 = 108. Naqshe mein bhi 3 C aur F ke jore wale hisse se thora bahar chhap gaya hai; uski asal jagah wohi lens hai. Doosri baat: only cricket ka sahi notation n(C) − n(C∩H) − n(C∩F) + n(C∩H∩F) hai, kitab wala n(C − C∩H∩F) nahin.

🧮 2. Application of set theory: counting formulas (p58)

Ab naqsha banaye baghair ginti. n(A) ka matlab hai set A ke elements ki tadaad. Union ke formula mein ghatana isi liye parta hai ke jo dono sets mein hain woh do dafa gin liye gaye, ek dafa n(A) mein aur ek dafa n(B) mein.

CaseFormulae (kitab p58)
(i) For any two overlapping sets A and Bn(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)
n(A − B) = n(A ∪ B) − n(B)
n(A − B) = n(A) − n(A ∩ B)
n(A ∪ B) = n(A − B) + n(A ∩ B) + n(B − A)
(ii) For any two sets A and B that are disjointn(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B)
n(A − B) = n(A)
(iii) For any three sets A, B and Cn(A ∪ B ∪ C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) − n(A ∩ B) − n(B ∩ C) − n(A ∩ C) + n(A ∩ B ∩ C)
⭐ Key Fact (p58), yahan se MCQ banti hai For any sets A and B:  A ∪ A = A,  A ∩ A = A,  A ∩ φ = φ,  A ∪ φ = A,  A ∩ B ⊆ A,  A ⊆ A ∪ B.
✏️ Example 13 (p58, p59), do sets ka formula In a class of 80 students, 40 like English, 34 like Mathematics and 9 like both. How many students like either of both subjects and how many like neither?
Total number of students = n(T) = 80  ·  n(E) = 40  ·  n(M) = 34  ·  n(E ∩ M) = 9
n(E ∪ M) = n(E) + n(M) − n(E ∩ M) = 40 + 34 − 9 = 65  (like either subject)
n(T) − n(E ∪ M) = 80 − 65 = 15  (like neither subject)
⚠️ Example 13 ke lafzon par dhyan Kitab likhti hai total number of students that like both subjects is n(E ∪ M) = 65. Yeh lafz ghalat hain. 65 woh hain jo either subject pasand karte hain (kam az kam ek). Both to sirf 9 hain, yaani n(E ∩ M). Isi tarah 15 woh hain jo neither subject pasand karte hain.
🧠 Pakki trick Sawal ke lafz seedha formula batate hain: both ya and = ∩ · either, or, at least one = ∪ · neither, none = n(U) − n(A ∪ B) · only A = n(A) − n(A ∩ B). Sirf teen sets wale formula mein signs ka order yaad rakho: plus, plus, plus, minus, minus, minus, plus. Ab Exercise 3.2 khud karo.
