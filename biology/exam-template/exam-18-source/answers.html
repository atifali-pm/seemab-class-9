SECTION A: ANSWER KEY (10 × 1 = 10 MARKS)
1. Inserting the human insulin gene into bacteria: (b) biotechnology 1
Biotechnology is the manipulation of living organisms, or their components, to make useful products. Genetically engineering bacteria so that they make human insulin is the book’s own example. Anatomy is internal structure, physiology is the working of organs, and pharmacy is not the branch that does the gene transfer.
Source: Ch 1, section 1.3 and Table 1.2 (Biotechnology), and Exercise Section I, MCQ 6, book p17. SLO 5 and SLO 6. NEW
2. Colonial organization is the unique feature of: (c) Protista 1
The book describes Kingdom Protista as eukaryotic organisms that are unicellular or colonial. Animalia and Plantae are multicellular, and Fungi are mostly multicellular with a chitin cell wall.
Source: Ch 2, section 2.4 (Four kingdoms of Domain Eukarya), and Exercise Section I, MCQ 12, book p29. SLO 4. NEW
3. Correct statement about ribosomes: (a) made in the nucleus, then work in the cytoplasm, where they make proteins 1
The subunits of the ribosome are assembled in the nucleolus inside the nucleus, and the ribosome then works in the cytoplasm. (b) is wrong because ribosomes are found in prokaryotic cells too. (c) is wrong because ribosomes are not bound by any membrane. (d) is wrong because a large number of ribosomes are scattered free in the cytoplasm.
Source: Ch 3, Ribosomes (book p34) and chapter Summary point 3 on the nucleolus (book p43), and Exercise Section I, MCQ 11, book p46. SLO 3. NEW
4. What halves the chromosome number in meiosis: (d) separation of homologous chromosomes during meiosis I 1
Meiosis I is the reduction division. In anaphase I the two members of each homologous pair go to opposite poles, so each pole gets a haploid set. DNA replication in interphase I doubles the DNA instead of halving it, crossing over only exchanges pieces, and the separation of sister chromatids in meiosis II keeps the number the same.
Source: Ch 4, section 4.5 (Meiosis I, Anaphase I), book p55, and Exercise Section I, MCQ 10, book p61. SLO 2 and SLO 3. RE-TEST of the mitosis versus meiosis mix-up in exam-17 Q.3(ii)(c).
5. Removes pathogens, fights infections and helps in healing: (b) immune system 1
Table 5.2 gives the immune system the homeostatic role “by removing pathogens, fighting infections and helping in healing”. The lymphatic system also defends the body, but its printed role in the same table is “maintain tissue fluid homeostasis”. That is the pair she must separate.
Source: Ch 5, Table 5.2, book p69 (lymphatic row) and p70 (immune row). SLO 4 and SLO 6. RE-TEST of exam-16 Q12, where she chose lymphatic instead of immune.
6. Structure at a different level of organization: (a) neuron 1
Kidney, liver and lung are all organs. A neuron is a single specialized cell, so it sits one level lower in the pattern of increasing complexity.
Source: Ch 5, section 5.1 levels of organization, cellular and organ levels (book p65 to p66), and Exercise Section I, MCQ 14, book p76. SLO 2. NEW
7. The disaccharide also called the transport sugar: (d) sucrose 1
Sucrose is glucose plus fructose. It is the sugar plants move around, and it is also cane sugar, the most familiar disaccharide. Maltose is malt sugar, lactose is milk sugar, and fructose is not a disaccharide at all.
Source: Ch 6, section 6.6.2 Disaccharides (book p85 to p86), and Exercise Section I, MCQ 9, book p92. SLO 6. NEW
8. All RNA nucleotides differ from DNA nucleotides in having a different: (c) pentose sugar 1
In RNA the pentose sugar is ribose; in DNA it is deoxyribose. That difference is in every single nucleotide. The nitrogen base differs only in one case, uracil in place of thymine, so it is not true of all of them. The phosphate group is the same, and there is no carboxylic group in a nucleotide.
Source: Ch 6, section 6.9 Structure of RNA, book p89, and Exercise Section I, MCQ 7, book p92. SLO 9. NEW
9. The two bonds shown by wavy lines in ATP: (a) high energy bonds 1
The book: “The covalent bonds between phosphate groups are called high energy or energy rich bonds. The two bonds linking the phosphate group together are represented by a specific sign of wavy lines.” Hydrogen bonds hold the two DNA strands together, a peptide bond joins two amino acids, and double bonds occur in unsaturated fatty acids.
Source: Ch 7, section 7.6.2 and Fig. 7.8, book p101. SLO 6. RE-TEST of exam-16 Q17, where she chose hydrogen bonds.
10. Products of the light reactions: (b) ATP, NADPH and oxygen 1
In the light dependent reactions the electron transport chain drives ATP synthesis, photosystem I reduces NADP+ to NADPH, and photolysis of water releases oxygen, which leaves the leaf through the stomata. NADP and PGA are not products of this phase. 3PGA belongs to the Calvin cycle.
Source: Ch 7, sections 7.7.1 and 7.7.1.1, book p102 to p104, and Exercise Section I, MCQ 14, book p110. SLO 7. NEW

Q.2 (Chapter 1) The spread of malaria 4
(i) Hypothesis (1): “Mosquitoes transmit Plasmodium”, that is, malaria is carried from a sick person to a healthy person by mosquito bite.
(ii) Ronald Ross’s sparrow experiment (2): Ross allowed female mosquitoes to bite sparrows that were suffering from malaria (1). After a few days the same mosquitoes were allowed to bite healthy sparrows, and those healthy sparrows became ill with malaria (1). Also accept the first Ross experiment: he allowed a female Anopheles to bite a malarial patient, dissected it, and found Plasmodium multiplying in the mosquito’s stomach.
(iii) Test on human beings (1): An Italian biologist let an Anopheles mosquito bite a malarial patient, kept the mosquito for a few days, and then allowed it to bite a healthy man. That man later developed malaria, which confirmed the hypothesis.
Source: Ch 1, section 1.8 (Spread of malaria) and Fig. 1.5, book p15 to p16. SLO 8. NEW STEMS, topic previously covered in exam-01, 07, 08 and 11.

Q.4 (Chapter 3) SER and the salivary gland cell 4
(i) A cell rich in SER (2), any two, 1 mark each:
  • It will synthesize lipids, including steroids, more efficiently.
  • It will detoxify harmful substances more efficiently.
  • If it is a muscle cell, it will carry out contraction more efficiently, because SER has an important role in that process.
  • Also accept: better mechanical support and better transport of substances within the cell, which the book gives for the ER as a whole.
(ii) Organelles abundant in a salivary gland cell (2), any two named with a reason, 1 mark each:
  • Ribosomes and rough endoplasmic reticulum: a gland cell makes large amounts of protein (the enzyme amylase), and protein synthesis happens on ribosomes attached to the RER.
  • Golgi apparatus: it stores, modifies and packages the material and carries out cell secretion, which is exactly what a gland does.
  • Mitochondria: making and secreting so much material needs a lot of ATP, and mitochondria are the power house of the cell.
Source: Ch 3, Endoplasmic reticulum and Ribosomes (book p34), Golgi apparatus and Mitochondria (book p35 to p36); the questions themselves are Exercise Section III Q3 and Exercise Section II Q11, book p46 to p47. SLO 3 and SLO 7. NEW

Q.6 (Chapter 6) Carbohydrates and chromatin 4
(i) Hydrates of carbon (2): Carbohydrates are compounds of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen in the ratio 1 : 2 : 1 (1 mark). Because the hydrogen and oxygen are present in the same proportion as in water, they are literally called hydrates of carbon, and their general formula is Cx(H2O)y (1 mark). For glucose this gives C6(H2O)6, that is C6H12O6.
(ii) Chromatin (2): Chromatin is composed of DNA together with proteins called histones (1 mark). The coiling of DNA around the histones forms bead like structures called nucleosomes (1 mark).
Source: Ch 6, section 6.5.1 Structure of carbohydrates (book p84 to p85) and section 6.8 with Fig. 6.14 (book p89); the wording is taken from Exercise Section II Q5 and Section III Q2, book p92. SLO 5, SLO 6 and SLO 8. NEW
Q.7 (Chapter 7) Respiration 4
(i) Glycolysis (2): Glycolysis takes place in the cytoplasm of the cell, and it is common to both aerobic and anaerobic respiration (1 mark). One molecule of glucose is oxidized into two molecules of pyruvic acid with a yield of two ATP molecules (1 mark). The book prints it as:
Glucose → 2 Pyruvic Acid + 2 ATP
Also accept the fuller form from section 7.8.3, which adds 2 NADH.
(ii) The four stages of aerobic respiration (2): glycolysis, formation of acetyl CoA, the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain, in that order (1.5 marks for all four in the right order). Oxygen is used in the electron transport chain, where it is the final electron acceptor and combines with hydrogen ions to form water (0.5 mark).
Source: Ch 7, section 7.8.1 (book p105), section 7.8.3 (book p106 to p107) and Key Points 13 and 16, book p108. SLO 8. Topic last touched in exam-02 (April); stems rewritten.

(i) Four differences between meiosis I and meiosis II (4), 1 mark each, any four:
Meiosis IMeiosis II

Prophase I is the longest phase of meiosisProphase II is short
(ii) The 18 chromosome cell (3), 1 mark each:
  • (a) At the end of meiosis I each cell has 9 chromosomes, because meiosis I halves the number.
  • (b) At the end of meiosis II each cell still has 9 chromosomes, because meiosis II separates the sister chromatids and does not reduce the number again.
  • (c) A total of four daughter cells is formed.
(iii) Why the daughter cells are not identical (1): because crossing over in prophase I exchanges genetic material between the non-sister chromatids of the homologous pair, so each chromosome now carries a new combination of genes. Also accept: the independent combination of the maternal and paternal chromosomes as they go to the poles in anaphase I.
