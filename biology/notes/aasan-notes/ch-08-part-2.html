🚚 Hissa 1: Transport kya hai aur kyun chahiye

8.2 TRANSPORT IN PLANTS (kitab p114)

Pauda apni jagah khara rehta hai, lekin us ke andar saara din traffic chalta hai: paani jarh se patton tak, khana patton se baqi plant tak, aur waste bahar. Isi saare aane jaane ka naam transport in plants hai.

📖 Kitab ke alfaaz (p114) You have learnt that plants absorb nutrients from the soil which are then utilized in the leaves to prepare organic nutrients by photosynthetic activity. These organic solutes are also to be moved to different parts of the plant body. When organic solutes are utilized in plant body, some waste compounds are also produced which are then removed out of the plant body. All these movements of materials are generally referred to as transport in plants.

Yani: mitti se nutrients andar aate hain, patton mein photosynthesis se organic nutrients bante hain, ye organic solutes plant ke doosre hisson tak jate hain, aur jo waste compounds bante hain woh bahar nikal diye jate hain. In saari movements ko mila kar transport in plants kehte hain.

8.2.1 Need of Transport (transport ki zaroorat, kitab p114)

📖 Kitab ke alfaaz (p114): teen levels In a plant body, transport occurs at three different levels:

At 1st level, the materials are moved from outside to inside the plant body because plant needs mineral nutrients and water to maintain its live activities which are to be absorbed from the soil, however, gases like CO2 and O2 are obtained from air.

At 2nd level, the materials are transported withing the plant body from one place to another because the inorganic nutrients must be transported to the leaves so that they can be used to prepare organic solutes by photosynthetic activity. These organic solutes are also to be moved to different parts of the plant body so that they can be used in plant growth and metabolism.

At 3rd level, the materials are moved from inside to outside the plant body because when organic solutes are utilized in plant body, some waste compounds are also produced which are then removed out of the plant body. These are the reasons why transport in plants occurs at three different levels.

1stOutside se insideMinerals aur paani mitti se, gases (CO2, O2) hawa se
2ndPlant ke andar andarNutrients patton tak, phir bana hua khana poore plant tak
3rdInside se outsideWaste compounds bahar nikalne ke liye
🧠 Trick Ghar ka darwaza socho: andar aao (1st), ghar mein ghoomo (2nd), bahar jao (3rd). Bas yehi teen levels hain.

🌱 Hissa 2: Jarh (root) ki banawat

8.2.2 Structure of root and root hairs (kitab p114, p115)

📖 Kitab ke alfaaz: root ki definition aur us ki do qismein Root is an underground part of the plant, which serve as the organ of uptake of mineral nutrients and water. It possesses large surface area due to extensive branching system and root hairs which make them favourable for organ of uptake.

Dicot plants have tap root which consists of a thick primary root that bears several secondary roots. Each secondary root further consists of large number of tertiary roots. On the other hand, monocot plants have fibrous adventitious roots that show no differentiation of primary, secondary, and tertiary roots.

Yani: root zameen ke neeche ka hissa hai aur organ of uptake hai (minerals aur paani lene wala). Us ki surface area do wajah se bari hai: extensive branching system aur root hairs. Geela kapra phaila do to jaldi sookhta hai, kyunke surface zyada mil jata hai; jarh bhi yehi karti hai.

Dicot plantsMonocot plants
Tap root: ek moti primary root, us par secondary roots, un par bohat se tertiary rootsFibrous adventitious roots: primary, secondary, tertiary ka koi differentiation nahi, sab barabar reshe

Internal structure of root and root hairs (jarh andar se, kitab p115)

📖 Kitab ka poora paragraph (p115) In in both monopod and dicot roots, the outermost layer is called epidermis. Many cells of the epidermis have extensions that are penetrated the spaces among soil particles. These extensions of the root epidermal cells are called root hairs, which increase approximately 67% surface area of the root. Inner to the epidermis is a ground issue, the cortex. The inner lining of the cortex is called the endodermis. The endodermal cells have deposition of a vax, the suberin in their radial walls. These depositions are in the form of strips called the Casparian Strips. Inner to the endodermis is the pericycle which surrounds the innermost vascular bundles. The vascular bundle consists of xylem and phloem which are arranged in the form of ring in monocot root with central ground tissue called as pith. Whereas, in the dicot roots, the vascular bundles are present in the form of star shape in the centre of the root without the presence of pith. Kitab mein "monopod" (monocot), "ground issue" (ground tissue) aur "a vax" (a wax) chhap gaya hai. Ye sirf typing ki ghaltiyan hain.

⚡ Hissa 3: Minerals ka uptake

8.2.2 Uptake of mineral salts and water by active and passive transport (kitab p115, p116)

⚠️ Kitab ki numbering ki ghalti Kitab ne do alag sections par ek hi number 8.2.2 chhap diya hai: p114 par "Structure of root and root hairs" aur p115 par ye wala. Number ki fikr mat karo, exam mein heading ka naam likhna.
📖 Kitab ke alfaaz (p115) Plant roots serve two vital functions: anchoring the plant in soil and absorbing essential minerals and water. To support the process of photosynthesis, plants require three key nutrients: carbon dioxide, water, minerals, and light. To obtain these nutrients, roots develop an extensive branching system and are covered by root hairs. These root hairs are the primary sites for the absorption of water and minerals.

With these raw materials (minerals and water from the soil, carbon dioxide from the air, and light energy), plants synthesize all the compounds they need. While most minerals enter the root hairs or epidermal cells of the roots through bulk flow with water, some are absorbed through diffusion, facilitated diffusion, or active transport.

  • Root ke do bunyadi kaam: plant ko mitti mein anchor karna, aur minerals aur paani absorb karna.
  • Root hairs hi absorption ki primary sites hain. Ye line MCQ ke liye sab se zaroori hai.
  • Zyada tar minerals paani ke saath bulk flow se andar aate hain; kuch diffusion, facilitated diffusion ya active transport se.
⚠️ Kitab ki ginti ki ghalti Kitab likhti hai "three key nutrients" magar phir chaar cheezein ginwati hai: carbon dioxide, water, minerals, and light. Exam mein chaaron likh dena aur ginti ka lafz na likhna.

Mechanism of mineral absorption by root (kitab p115)

📖 Kitab ke alfaaz (p115) Plants absorb minerals from water in the soil. When minerals are stuck to soil particles and not dissolved in water, plants can't use them. Only those minerals can be absorbed that are dissolved in soil water. Plants take in minerals through root cells using both passive and active processes that require energy, specifically ATP.

Yani: minerals mitti ke paani se milte hain. Jo minerals soil particles ke saath chipke hue aur ghule hue nahi, woh plant istemal nahi kar sakta. Sirf dissolved minerals hi absorb hote hain.

⚠️ Ek confusing jumla Aakhri line se lagta hai ke dono processes ko ATP chahiye. Aisa nahi hai, aur kitab khud agle page par saaf kar deti hai: ATP sirf active transport mein lagta hai. Passive (diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis) mein koi energy kharch nahi hoti.

Teen tareeqe: diffusion, facilitated diffusion, active transport (kitab p116)

📖 Kitab ke teen paragraph (p116)

1. Passive uptake: Passive uptake happens through diffusion, as minerals move along their concentration gradient through plasmodesmata to cells in the root's cortex, endodermis, and pericycle before reaching the xylem cells. They are then pulled up by transpiration.

2. Facilitated diffusion: Certain nutrients are transported from the soil into the epidermal cells of roots through their cell membranes by a process called facilitated diffusion. In facilitated diffusion, proteins within the cell membrane act as carrier molecules to help move nutrients across the membrane. These carrier proteins are found in the cell membranes of epidermal and other root cells.

3. Active transport: Active transport, on the other hand, requires energy and allows plants to take in minerals even when their concentration inside the root cells is higher than in the soil. This is against the natural concentration gradient, and it involves the use of ATP. Active transport is selective and relies on respiration. Some ions can also move through passive transport.

📌 Science Titbits (kitab p116, side box) One crucial nutrient, nitrogen (N), is often scarce in both rock particles and soil water. Many plants have developed beneficial partnerships with other organisms to obtain these limited nutrients. For example, mycorrhizal fungi and nitrogen-fixing bacteria in the root nodules of legumes are examples of such partnerships. Fungal associations with the roots of higher plants enhance the plant's ability to absorb minerals like phosphorus and trace metals such as zinc and copper. A root infected with mycorrhizal fungi can absorb phosphate at a faster rate than an uninfected root. In exchange for their services, mycorrhizal fungi receive sugars and protection from the plant, ultimately increasing the plant's efficiency in acquiring mineral nutrients. Mycorrhizae form associations with plants from a wide range of plant families, covering approximately 90% of flowering plants.

Yani: nitrogen mitti mein aksar kam hota hai, is liye plants mycorrhizal fungi aur legumes ki root nodules wale nitrogen-fixing bacteria se partnership bana lete hain. Fungi plant ko phosphorus, zinc aur copper lene mein madad dete hain aur badle mein sugars aur protection lete hain. Ye rishta taqreeban 90% flowering plants mein milta hai.

💧 Hissa 4: Paani ka uptake

Mechanism of water absorption by root (kitab p116, p117)

📖 Kitab ke alfaaz (p116 se p117): exam mein yehi likho Absorption of water by roots occur by means of a passive transport mechanism called osmosis. This is the movement of water molecules through a partially permeable membrane from an area where they are more concentrated to an area where they are less concentrated. If water moves into a cell through osmosis, it's called endosmosis, and if it moves out of the cell, it's called exosmosis.

In the epidermal cells of roots, the cell wall allows water and minerals to pass through freely. However, the cell membrane is selectively permeable, meaning it only allows certain substances from the solution to pass through. Water entering the epidermal cells follows the concentration gradient, moving through the cortex, endodermis, pericycle, and finally into the xylem cells.

Yani: paani osmosis se andar aata hai, aur osmosis ek passive tareeqa hai, is mein ATP kharch nahi hota. Osmosis ka matlab hai paani ka partially permeable membrane ke through zyada concentrated jagah se kam concentrated jagah ki taraf move karna.

Section III: Extensive Answer Questions (kitab p128)

✏️ Q2: Explain the internal structure of root and root hair.

Introduction: Root plant ka underground hissa hai jo minerals aur paani ka organ of uptake hai. Is ki surface area extensive branching system aur root hairs ki wajah se bohat bari hoti hai. Monocot aur dicot, dono roots mein bahar se andar ki tarteeb ye hai:

  1. Epidermis: sab se bahar wali layer (figures mein epiblema).
  2. Root hairs: epidermal cells ki extensions jo mitti ke zarron ke darmiyan ki jagahon mein ghus jati hain. Ye surface area approximately 67% barha deti hain.
  3. Cortex: epidermis ke andar ka ground tissue.
  4. Endodermis: cortex ki inner lining. Is ke cells ki radial walls par suberin naam ka wax strips ki shakal mein jamta hai, jinhein Casparian Strips kehte hain.
  5. Pericycle: endodermis ke andar, jo sab se andar wale vascular bundles ko gher leta hai.
  6. Vascular bundle: xylem aur phloem. Monocot mein ring ki shakal aur beech mein pith; dicot mein centre par star shape aur pith nahi.

Root hair ki apni banawat (Figure 8.4): root hair asal mein root epithelial (epiblemal) cell ki lambi extension hai. Is mein cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, ek bara vacuole aur mitochondria hote hain.

Function: cell wall paani aur minerals ko aazadi se guzarne deti hai, jabke cell membrane selectively permeable hai. Mitochondria respiration se ATP dete hain jo active transport mein kaam aata hai. Isi liye root hairs paani aur minerals ke absorption ki primary sites hain.

Saath mein Figure 8.3 (T.S. of monocot root aur dicot root) aur Figure 8.4 (structure of root hair) ke labelled diagram zaroor bana dena.

✏️ Q3: Describe how roots take up water and mineral salts by active and passive absorption.

Introduction: Root ke do bunyadi kaam hain: plant ko mitti mein anchor karna aur minerals aur paani absorb karna. Root hairs hi is absorption ki primary sites hain. Absorption do tarah se hoti hai: passive (energy ke baghair) aur active (ATP kharch kar ke).

(A) Mineral salts ka uptake

  1. Pehli shart: jo minerals soil particles ke saath chipke hue aur paani mein ghule nahi, unhein plant istemal nahi kar sakta. Sirf wohi minerals absorb hote hain jo soil water mein dissolved hon.
  2. Bulk flow: zyada tar minerals root hairs ya epidermal cells mein paani ke saath dakhil hote hain.
  3. Passive uptake (diffusion): minerals apne concentration gradient ke saath plasmodesmata ke zariye cortex, endodermis aur pericycle ke cells se hote hue xylem cells tak pohanchte hain, jahan se unhein transpiration upar kheench leti hai. Energy kharch nahi hoti.
  4. Facilitated diffusion: kuch nutrients epidermal cells ke cell membranes ke through aate hain. Membrane ke proteins carrier molecules ki tarah kaam kar ke unhein paar karwate hain. Ye carrier proteins epidermal aur baqi root cells ke membranes mein hote hain. Is mein bhi energy kharch nahi hoti.
  5. Active transport: is mein energy chahiye. Is se plant minerals tab bhi le leta hai jab un ki concentration root cells ke andar mitti se zyada ho. Ye against the natural concentration gradient hai aur is mein ATP lagta hai. Ye selective hai aur respiration par depend karta hai. Kuch ions passive transport se bhi move kar jate hain.

(B) Water ka uptake

  1. Osmosis: paani ka absorption ek passive transport mechanism se hota hai jise osmosis kehte hain, yani paani ke molecules ka partially permeable membrane ke through zyada concentrated se kam concentrated jagah ki taraf jana.
  2. Endosmosis aur exosmosis: paani cell ke andar aaye to endosmosis, bahar jaye to exosmosis.
  3. Cell wall aur cell membrane: epidermal cells mein cell wall paani aur minerals ko aazadi se guzarne deti hai, jabke cell membrane selectively permeable hai.
  4. Rasta: paani concentration gradient ke saath cortex, endodermis, pericycle se hota hua xylem cells tak pohanch jata hai.

Conclusion: Is tarah root paani aur ghule hue mineral salts dono absorb kar leti hai: paani hamesha passive tareeqe (osmosis) se, aur minerals kabhi passive (diffusion, facilitated diffusion) to kabhi active transport se, jis mein ATP kharch hota hai.
