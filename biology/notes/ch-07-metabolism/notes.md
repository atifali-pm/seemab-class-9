## Topic 1 — Metabolism and Its Types

### Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Metabolism** | The sum of all chemical reactions occurring within each cell of a living organism for the maintenance and sustainability of life |
| **Catabolism** | Biochemical reactions that break down complex molecules into simpler ones; energy is released |
| **Anabolism** | Biochemical reactions that build complex molecules from simpler ones; energy is consumed |
| **Biochemical reactions** | Chemical reactions taking place inside living organisms |

### Catabolism vs Anabolism

| Feature | Catabolism | Anabolism |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Complex → Simple | Simple → Complex |
| Energy | Released | Consumed |
| Example 1 | Digestion (large food → small molecules) | Photosynthesis (CO₂ + H₂O → glucose) |
| Example 2 | Cellular respiration (glucose → CO₂ + H₂O) | Glycogenesis (glucose → glycogen) |

> **Key point:** Energy released in catabolism is utilized in anabolism.

## Topic 2 — Enzymes and Their Characteristics

### Definition

> **Enzymes** are biologically active globular proteins made by living cells, which tremendously speed up biochemical reactions.

- Enzymes = biological catalysts
- Substrates = molecules enzymes act on
- Products = substrates modified by enzymes
- Study of enzymes = **enzymology**

### Types of Enzymes

| Type | Location | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intracellular** | Work inside cells | Enzymes of glycolysis (cytoplasm) |
| **Extracellular** | Released to work outside | Pepsin (stomach cavity) |

### 8 Characteristics of Enzymes

| # | Characteristic | Key detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Globular proteins** | Chains of amino acids folded into 3D globular shape; some are RNA (ribozymes) |
| 2 | **Increase reaction rate** | Speed up reactions millions of times; do NOT start reactions |
| 3 | **Required in small quantity** | Regain original shape after reaction; used repeatedly |
| 4 | **Specific** | Each enzyme catalyses only one type of reaction (one substrate) |
| 5 | **Require cofactor** | Many need non-protein component for proper function |
| 6 | **Regulated** | Activity can be increased or decreased by cell according to requirements |
| 7 | **Make metabolic pathways** | Work in sequence; metabolic pathway = series of connected reactions |
| 8 | **Lower activation energy** | Reduce energy needed to start a reaction so it happens at body temperature |

### Cofactors

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Activator** | Detachable inorganic ions | Zn²⁺, Fe²⁺, Cu²⁺, chloride ions |
| **Prosthetic group** | Organic molecule tightly/permanently attached to enzyme | FAD (Flavin Adenine Dinucleotide), Haem group |
| **Coenzyme** | Detachable organic molecule | NAD, Coenzyme A, Vitamin A, Vitamin C |

## Topic 3 — Mechanism of Enzyme Action

### Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Active site** | Small, charged-bearing cavity on enzyme surface where substrate binds |
| **ES Complex** | Enzyme-Substrate Complex; formed when substrate binds to active site |
| **EP Complex** | Enzyme-Product Complex; ES complex after catalysis occurs |

### Steps of Enzyme Action

```
Enzyme + Substrate → ES Complex → EP Complex → Products + Enzyme (unchanged)
```

1. Substrate binds to active site → forms ES Complex
2. Enzyme catalyses conversion of substrate to product → forms EP Complex
3. EP Complex breaks apart → products released, enzyme released unchanged
4. Enzyme is available again for the same reaction

> **Activation energy:** The minimum extra energy needed to start a reaction. Enzymes lower this so reactions occur at body temperature.

## Topic 4 — Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

### 4.1 Temperature

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Low temperature | Enzyme inactive (low activation energy available); activity resumes at normal temperature |
| Increasing temperature | Rate of reaction increases |
| **Optimum temperature** | Maximum activity (37°C for human enzymes) |
| High temperature (>50°C) | Enzyme **denatured** — active site damaged, activity lost permanently |

### 4.2 pH

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Below optimum pH | Rate of reaction decreases |
| **Optimum pH** | Maximum activity |
| Above optimum pH | Rate of reaction decreases |
| Extreme pH change | Enzyme **denatured** |

| Enzyme | Location | Optimum pH |
|---|---|---|
| Pepsin | Stomach | pH 2.0 (acidic) |
| Trypsin | Small intestine | pH 8.0 (alkaline) |

### 4.3 Substrate Concentration

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Low substrate, many enzymes | Rate proportional to substrate concentration |
| Increasing substrate | Rate of reaction increases |
| All active sites occupied | Rate plateaus — **saturation of active site** |
| Further increase in substrate | No further effect |

### Inhibitors and Activators

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Inhibitors** | Molecules that stop enzymatic reactions |
| **Activators** | Molecules that increase enzymatic reaction rate |

## Topic 5 — Enzyme Inhibition

### Definition

**Enzyme inhibition:** phenomenon where enzymes cannot perform their role due to interference of chemicals at the reaction site.

**Inhibitor:** a chemical substance that reacts with enzyme in place of substrate but does not convert to products, thus inhibiting enzyme action.

### Competitive vs Non-Competitive Inhibition

| Feature | Competitive | Non-Competitive |
|---|---|---|
| Resembles substrate? | Yes — similar shape | No — different shape |
| Binds at? | **Active site** | **Allosteric site** (different from active site) |
| Reversible? | Yes — if substrate concentration increased | No — damages active site permanently |
| Mechanism | Blocks substrate from binding | Distorts enzyme shape → active site damaged |
| Result | No product formed | No product formed |

> **Allosteric site:** the binding point for non-competitive inhibitors, located away from the active site.

## Topic 6 — ATP: The Energy Currency of the Cell

### Definition

> **ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)** is the universal energy currency of cells — an energy-rich compound that stores and releases energy on demand.

### Structure of ATP

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| Adenine | Double-ringed nitrogen base |
| Ribose | Five-carbon sugar |
| Phosphate groups | Three phosphate groups (PO₄) linked in a chain = **triphosphate group** |

**Assembly:**
```
Adenine + Ribose → Adenosine
Adenosine + 1 PO₄ → AMP (Adenosine Monophosphate)
AMP + 1 PO₄ → ADP (Adenosine Diphosphate)
ADP + 1 PO₄ → ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)
```

**High-energy bonds:** The covalent bonds between phosphate groups store energy (shown as wavy lines ~P).

### ATP–ADP Cycle

```
ATP + H₂O  →  ADP + Pᵢ + ENERGY  (hydrolysis — energy released for cell work)
ADP + Pᵢ + ENERGY  →  ATP        (condensation — energy stored from catabolism/light)
```

> **Science Titbit:** Human cells hydrolyse 100–150 moles of ATP per day. One glucose molecule in aerobic respiration produces a net 36 ATP molecules.

## Topic 7 — Photosynthesis

### Overall Equation

```
6CO₂ + 12H₂O  →(Light, Chlorophyll)→  C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6H₂O + 6O₂
```

### Two Phases

| Phase | Location | Needs light? | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Light-dependent reactions** | Thylakoid membranes (grana) | Yes | Water split (photolysis), ATP & NADPH produced, O₂ released |
| **Light-independent reactions** (Calvin cycle / dark reactions) | Stroma of chloroplast | No | CO₂ fixed using ATP & NADPH to make glucose (G3P) |

### Light-Dependent Reactions — Steps

1. Photosystem II absorbs light → electrons emitted from chlorophyll a
2. Water undergoes **photolysis**: H₂O → O₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ (replaces lost electrons)
3. Oxygen released through stomata
4. Electrons pass through **Electron Transport Chain (ETC)** → energy used for ATP synthesis
5. Photosystem I absorbs light → electrons reduce NADP⁺ to **NADPH**

### Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle) — Steps

1. CO₂ combines with **RuBP** (5-carbon) → unstable 6-carbon intermediate
2. 6-carbon molecule splits → two **3-PGA** (3-carbon) molecules
3. 3-PGA reduced by NADPH + ATP → **G3P** (3-carbon sugar)
4. Some G3P → glucose; some G3P regenerates RuBP

### Limiting Factors of Photosynthesis

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Light intensity | More light → faster photosynthesis |
| CO₂ concentration | More CO₂ → faster (up to a point) |
| Temperature | Optimum = 25°C; too high or too low reduces rate |

## Topic 8 — Respiration

### Overall Equation (Aerobic)

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂  →  6CO₂ + 6H₂O + 36 ATP
```

### Glycolysis (common to both aerobic and anaerobic)

```
Glucose → 2 Pyruvic Acid + 2 ATP + 2 NADH
```
- Location: **cytoplasm**
- Occurs in: both aerobic and anaerobic respiration

### Anaerobic Respiration

> Respiration without oxygen. Incomplete oxidation of glucose.

| Type | Reaction | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Alcoholic fermentation** | Pyruvic acid → Ethyl alcohol + CO₂ | Yeast, some bacteria |
| **Lactic acid fermentation** | Pyruvic acid → Lactic acid | Muscle cells during vigorous exercise; bacteria |

### Aerobic Respiration — 4 Stages

| Stage | Location | Inputs | Outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Glycolysis | Cytoplasm | Glucose | 2 Pyruvic acid, 2 ATP, 2 NADH |
| 2. Formation of Acetyl CoA | Mitochondria | 2 Pyruvic acid | 2 Acetyl CoA, 2 CO₂, 2 NADH |
| 3. Krebs Cycle | Mitochondrial matrix | 2 Acetyl CoA | 4CO₂, 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂ |
| 4. Electron Transport Chain | Inner mitochondrial membrane | NADH, FADH₂, O₂ | 32 ATP, H₂O |

**Net ATP from aerobic respiration:** 36 ATP per glucose molecule

### Aerobic vs Anaerobic Comparison

| Feature | Aerobic | Anaerobic |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen needed? | Yes | No |
| Glucose fully broken down? | Yes | No |
| Final products | CO₂ + H₂O | CO₂ + alcohol OR lactic acid |
| ATP yield | 36 ATP | 2 ATP |
| Location | Cytoplasm + mitochondria | Cytoplasm only |

## Must-Remember Key Points

- Metabolism = catabolism + anabolism
- Enzymes = biological catalysts; they lower activation energy but are not consumed
- Active site = where substrate binds; specific to ONE substrate type
- Optimum temperature for human enzymes = 37°C; denaturation occurs at high temperatures
- Cofactors: activators (inorganic ions), prosthetic groups (permanent organic), coenzymes (detachable organic)
- Competitive inhibition: reversible, blocks active site; non-competitive: irreversible, blocks allosteric site
- ATP = Adenosine + 3 phosphate groups; energy stored in high-energy phosphate bonds
- Photosynthesis equation: 6CO₂ + 12H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6H₂O + 6O₂
- Aerobic respiration equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + 36 ATP
- Glycolysis (cytoplasm) is common to BOTH aerobic and anaerobic respiration
- Krebs cycle is in the mitochondrial matrix; ETC is on the inner mitochondrial membrane
